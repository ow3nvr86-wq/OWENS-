Six months after Pearl Harbor, Japan's navy looked unstoppable.
Then, in a few minutes, it lost three aircraft carriers.
Japan planned a trap at Midway, a tiny island in the Pacific.
But American codebreakers had read its secret messages.
On June 4th, 1942, the two fleets met.
American torpedo planes attacked first, and were nearly wiped out.
But they pulled Japan's fighters down to the waves.
Then dive bombers came out of the clouds.
Three Japanese carriers were hit, and burning, within minutes.
A fourth fought on, but it was sunk too.
Japan never recovered its edge.
The tide of the Pacific war had turned.
