WORLD WAR II IN TWO PARTS - PART 2: HOW IT ALL FELL APART (1942-1945)
Target 34-39 s. About 89 spoken words (numbers counted as said). Same brisk pace as Part 1.

HOOK OPTIONS
  A. "Part two. Hitler's empire looked unstoppable. Then, in one frozen city, it began to break."   <- CHOSEN (picks
     up Part 1's cliffhanger and opens a new question at once)
  B. "Part two. In December 1941, Germany and Japan looked unbeatable. Four years later, both had surrendered."
  C. "Part two. The turning point of World War Two came down to one city, in the snow."

SCRIPT
0-5    Part two. Hitler's empire looked unstoppable. Then, in one frozen city, it began to break.
5-10   Stalingrad, 1943: an entire German army surrenders.
10-13  America fights back across the Pacific.
13-21  June 6th, 1944. D-Day. About 156,000 Allied troops land in Normandy in a single day.
21-28  Germany is crushed from both sides. In May 1945, with Soviet troops in Berlin, it surrenders.
28-33  In August, two atomic bombs fall on Japan. By September, the war is over.
33-37  An estimated 70 to 85 million people died.

SAY IT AS: "nineteen forty-three", "June sixth, nineteen forty-four", "a hundred and fifty-six thousand",
"nineteen forty-five", "seventy to eighty-five million".

ANIMATION PLAN (maps throughout)
- Hook: the Part 1 map, at its largest; snow; the camera dives to Stalingrad on the Volga.
- Stalingrad: Soviet arrows close a ring around the city; the German symbol shatters; "FEB 1943".
- Pacific: the camera slides across the globe; US arrows hop island to island towards Japan.
- D-Day: back to Europe; the Channel fills with landing craft; five beach names light up (Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno,
  Sword); "156,000" counts up.
- Collapse: red arrows from the east and blue from the west squeeze the grey back into Germany; they meet; Berlin
  flashes; "MAY 8, 1945".
- Japan: the globe turns; two flashes over Hiroshima and Nagasaki, shown with restraint (no casualties drawn);
  "SEPT 2, 1945".
- Ending: the map clears to its post-war shape; "70-85 MILLION" in quiet white; a slow fade.

FACT NOTES (sources in sources.txt)
- Stalingrad: the German Sixth Army surrendered on 31 Jan to 2 Feb 1943 (about 91,000 prisoners).
- D-Day: about 156,000 troops landed on 6 June 1944 (some counts are nearly 160,000).
- Germany surrendered on 7-8 May 1945; Japan announced its surrender on 15 Aug and signed it on 2 Sept 1945.
- Deaths: estimates run from 70 to 85 million, soldiers and civilians.
