AREA 51 - RESEARCH SOURCES, FACT TABLE AND HONESTY NOTES
============================================================

Every date, number and name used in the narration or on screen is listed below with the sources that support it.
Confirmed history is stated as fact. UFO reports are presented as reports, not as evidence of anything. The video
does not claim that aliens or alien technology have ever been found at Area 51, because no reliable evidence says so.

4. CONFIRMED vs. DISPUTED vs. SPECULATION
-----------------------------------------
CONFIRMED (official records): the CIA selected Groom Lake in 1955 to test the U-2; the A-12 OXCART was tested there
from 1962; the facility's existence and name were officially acknowledged in the CIA history released in August
2013; the CIA's own historians wrote that U-2 test flights caused "a tremendous increase" in UFO reports and that
Air Force Project Blue Book investigators checked sightings against U-2 flight logs; the site was later used for
other classified aircraft programmes.

DISPUTED: the CIA estimate (Haines 1997, citing CIA officials) that U-2 and OXCART flights accounted for more than
half of all UFO reports in the late 1950s and most of the 1960s. Researchers on both sides of the UFO debate have
challenged that figure (see SPACE). The video therefore does NOT use the "more than half" number; it says only that
the spy planes caused a surge in reports, which is the CIA history's own wording.

SPECULATION / RUMOUR (not supported by evidence and not presented as fact): that alien spacecraft or bodies are kept
at Area 51, or that alien technology has been reverse-engineered there. AARO (2024) found no evidence for this.
The video mentions UFO stories only as reports and rumours, and the observer's light in scene 4 is deliberately left
unexplained: it is a dramatisation of what witnesses described, not a depiction of a real object.

5. WHAT IS ILLUSTRATIVE (RECREATION, NOT RECORD)
------------------------------------------------
- The base: runway, hangars, buildings, fences and guard towers are a recreation for the film, not a plan of the
  real site. The perimeter scene is a composite (the real boundary is marked mainly by posts and signs; the fence,
  gate and tower are added for the picture).
- The warning sign recreates wording widely photographed at the boundary; it is not a copy of a specific sign.
- The aircraft are code-built models from published dimensions and photographs: the U-2 in its early unpainted
  natural-metal finish (as in 1955), the A-12 in its later all-black paint.
- The newspapers use fictional mastheads and generic headlines in the style of 1950s UFO coverage; no specific
  article is reproduced and no quote is invented.
- The CIA history's cover is a recreation (its title, authors and date are real); the documents, the sketch map and
  the "photographs" (code-drawn plan views) are illustrative and labelled as such where legible.
- The observer and the light in the sky are a dramatisation.

6. FACTS CHECKED BUT CUT FOR TIME
---------------------------------
- Names: the site was also called "Paradise Ranch", "the Ranch", "Watertown" and "Dreamland"; "Area 51" comes from
  its grid designation on Atomic Energy Commission maps.
- The Air Force acknowledged an "operating location near Groom Lake" in the 1990s, but not the name "Area 51".
- Later programmes at the site included the F-117 stealth fighter and tests of captured Soviet MiG fighters (NSA-1).
- 2025: the Wall Street Journal reported that some officials deliberately fed UFO stories to cover classified
  aircraft (WSJ). Not used in the narration because it concerns the 1980s and later, outside the story's span.
