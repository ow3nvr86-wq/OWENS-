WHAT IF YOU WERE THE ONLY MAN ALIVE? - PART 2
A 30-second vertical Short, made entirely with code
===================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 30.0 s, H.264 + AAC. The sequel to Part 1, told in the second person ("you"). You are the
only man alive - and now the entire world is looking for you. On an eerily quiet street a giant screen switches on with
your face: THE ENTIRE WORLD IS SEARCHING FOR YOU. The news, the giant screens, a square of thousands, a government
situation room and a lab all want answers. A black government car takes you to an enormous research facility - light
lines across a dark floor, walls of displays, a holographic globe - where a scientist welcomes you into a glass room. A
timeline of the planet shows billions vanishing at the same instant, and your photo, highlighted: ANOMALY - SUBJECT 001.
She turns to you, the lights dim, a symbol nobody understands fills the wall, the picture glitches: PART 3?

Everything you see and hear is generated by code:
- People: the procedural human builder from Part 1 (build_human in animation.py). The main character is the SAME
  figure as in Part 1, built from the same specification (face, skin, eyes, short dark strand hair, stubble, grey
  T-shirt, dark sweatpants, height and proportions); the only change is a pair of white trainers, because he goes
  outside. His ID portrait on the screens is the same portrait as Part 1. Scientists, the facility director, the news
  presenter, officials, staff and crowds are built with the same builder; crowds are baked and instanced by geometry
  nodes.
- Sets, lights and cameras: Blender's Python API (bpy). No stock footage, downloaded models, textures or templates.
- Screens: tools/textures.py (Pillow) draws the giant screen's search board, the urgent government report, the
  disappearance data, the planet-wide timeline (and its ANOMALY version) and the symbol, plus the Part 1 graphics.
- Captions: tools/captions.py (Pillow): word-by-word captions plus five large key captions.
- Music and sound: numpy synthesis in animation.py --stage audio. No samples or recordings.
- Narration: a synthetic voice (Kokoro-82M, voice "bm_george", the same narrator as Part 1), made by tools/voiceover.py.


FILES
-----
  script.txt          the narration (76 words) with measured start/end times
  hooks.txt           three hooks with scores, the selected visual-first hook, retention notes and the cliffhanger
  storyboard.txt      scene-by-scene storyboard (generated from scene_timing.json by tools/voiceover.py)
  animation.py        the whole production in one Blender script: people, sets, animation, cameras, lighting, render,
                      audio, edit and encode (see "HOW animation.py IS ORGANISED")
  subtitles.srt       subtitles timed from the synthesised voice (max 3 words)
  sound_design.txt    the sound idea, every cue with its time, and a music guide for re-scoring
  sources.txt         claim table, what is fiction, what was checked
  README.txt          this file
  final_video.mp4     the finished Short

  scene_timing.json   the single source of truth for timing (scenes, shots, narration, titles, key captions)
  render_all.sh       resumable full render in chunks
  tools/              voiceover.py, captions.py, textures.py, contact.py
  audio/              narration.wav, mix_with_voice.wav (final mix), mix_no_voice.wav (music + sound only)
  renders/            preview.mp4 (low-resolution preview), final_video_no_voice.mp4 (no narration or captions)
  assets/fonts/       Anton, Old Standard TT, Covered By Your Grace (SIL OFL)


HOW animation.py IS ORGANISED (bpy)
-----------------------------------
- Collections: City (street, his tower, the giant screen, the government car), Chars (the main character), Square,
  News, Lab, Sit (situation room), Faci (the research facility and the glass research room), Booth (portrait booth),
  Flat (Part 1's flat, part of the tower), FX, Rig (cameras), Lights (sun), PeopleT (baked crowd people, instanced).
  Each shot shows only the collections it needs (SHOTCFG), which keeps every frame cheap to render.
- Reusable functions: build_human, head_mesh, hair_strands, lips_object, bake_human, people / instancer (crowds),
  build_city, build_vehicle, build_square, build_newsroom, build_sitroom, build_lab, build_facility, screen_mat,
  glow_img, panel_metal, camera_shot (one camera per shot on a keyed path with a moving target, bound by a timeline
  marker), pose, walk_along, reach, look_at, blend_face, landmark, flicker_on, update_world.
- Animation is data-driven: a frame_change_pre handler (on_frame) applies the shot's look, poses the people
  (CHOR / CHOR_PEOPLE), drives the car, the hologram, the screen textures and the lights (update_world).

HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3), ffmpeg, Python 3 with Pillow and numpy. The voice step needs Python
with kokoro-onnx and soundfile, plus kokoro-v1.0.onnx and voices-v1.0.bin. In this repository's Claude Code cloud
sessions the SessionStart hook installs everything and exports $BLENDER, $SHORTS_PY, $KOKORO_PY and $KOKORO_DIR.

  cd WHAT_IF_YOU_WERE_THE_ONLY_MAN_ALIVE_PART_2
  $SHORTS_PY tools/textures.py                      # screen graphics -> build/tex/ (animation.py also runs it if missing)
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, storyboard.txt
  $SHORTS_PY tools/captions.py                      # caption images (the edit stage also runs it if missing)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 23,69,102,141,369,417,510,606,738,840
  ./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
  ./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/ (resumable)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                  # -> final_video.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice       # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4

His ID portrait (build/tex/portrait.png) is rendered from the 3D character before the first frame if it is missing
(build/tex/feeds_ok marks it done). For exact continuity this production reuses Part 1's portrait of the same figure.

To edit in CapCut instead: render the frames, run the audio stage, import renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4 (or the
frames plus audio/mix_no_voice.wav), add audio/narration.wav and use subtitles.srt for the captions.

RENDER TIMES (4 CPU cores, no GPU, Cycles CPU)
----------------------------------------------
  Look-dev stills (270x480, 4 samples)         about 3-6 s per frame, plus about 1.5-2 min of scene building per run
                                               (the people, their strand hair and the baked crowds are built every run)
  Preview, 900 frames (270x480, 4 samples)     33 min (about 2 s per frame, including scene building per chunk)
  Final, 900 frames (540x960, 6 samples)       about 2 h 40 min of rendering, about 8.3 s per frame on average. It ran
                                               as two background runs (the first stopped at the 2-hour task limit
                                               after 781 frames and was resumed); 165 frames of three shots (the
                                               square, the lab, the facility crane) were re-rendered after QC.
  Audio stage                                  about 10 s
  Edit stage (final_video.mp4)                 2 min 15 s
  Edit stage --no-voice                        2 min 1 s

  final_video.mp4                   1080x1920, 30 fps, 30.0 s, H.264 + AAC 48 kHz, 29.3 MB, -13.7 LUFS (peak -2.7 dBFS)
  renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4  1080x1920, 30 fps, 30.0 s, H.264 + AAC 48 kHz, 29.8 MB, -14.0 LUFS (peak -1.5 dBFS)


QUALITY CHECK (against the brief)
---------------------------------
- Same main character as Part 1: the same build_human specification (only trainers added) and the same ID portrait.
- Visual-first hook (Hook 2): the giant screen switches on with his face and THE ENTIRE WORLD IS SEARCHING FOR YOU. at
  1.6-2.0 s, a fast push to the screen, then a cut to him looking up.
- All five scenes as briefed; camera movement in every one of the 16 shots; cool blue, grey and subtle purple grade.
- Bigger than Part 1: a square of thousands, an enormous facility (56 x 74 m hall, 20 m high) with a holographic globe,
  and a planet-wide timeline.
- Key captions: THE WORLD IS SEARCHING. / BILLIONS ARE WATCHING. / SCIENTISTS WANT ANSWERS. / SOMETHING IS DIFFERENT. /
  PART 3?. Word captions: Anton, uppercase, white with a black outline, active word amber, max 3 words, about 64% down.
  The captions match the narration word for word (76 words).
- Sound: news ambience, notifications, low bass, the vehicle, footsteps, electronic doors, interface sounds, rising
  suspense and a digital glitch at the end, ducked under the voice; -14 LUFS.
- The ending does not reveal the answer.


LIMITATIONS (what this code can and cannot do)
---------------------------------------------
- The people are procedural, built only from Python and shapes - no scanned faces, hand sculpting or facial rig. They
  are closer to real people than mannequins (proportions, irises, lids, lips, strand hair, subsurface skin) but faces
  are smooth and simplified, clothes are shaped shells rather than simulated cloth, and joints are rotated parts rather
  than a skinned skeleton, so shoulders and elbows can look segmented close up. Shots are framed so faces read best.
- The handshake and gestures are posed with simple inverse kinematics (reach), not motion capture; hands do not grip.
- Crowds are baked, static people that turn slightly; they do not walk.
- The hologram is an emissive wireframe; the glass is a cheap transparent/glossy mix (no refraction) to keep render
  times low; there is no motion blur (it made the CPU render far slower).
- The car is a simple rounded body with blue light strips and a sliding door, not a model of a real vehicle.
- Sound was checked by loudness measurement and timing only.


ACCURACY AND HONESTY
--------------------
- Fiction, labelled on screen ("PART 2 - A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO"). The cause of the disappearance is never stated.
- The only real-world-scaled figure is "ABOUT 4 BILLION GONE" on a screen, which matches the real number of men on Earth
  (roughly 4.1 billion; see sources.txt). Everything else on the screens is an invented prop.
- The channel, places, organisations, the symbol and the people are invented; nobody real is depicted.
