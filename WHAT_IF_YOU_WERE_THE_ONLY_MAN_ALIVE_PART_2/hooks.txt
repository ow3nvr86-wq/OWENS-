WHAT IF YOU WERE THE ONLY MAN ALIVE? PART 2 - OPENING HOOKS AND ENDING
=====================================================================

Three hooks were written for Part 2. Scores are this production's own assessment (25 = best), weighing how fast the
first two seconds grab, how clearly they continue Part 1, and whether the picture works with the sound off.

HOOK 1  (verbal)  "Every camera on Earth is pointed at one man. You."
        Punchy and personal, but it depends on the words: with the sound off, the first frames would be an ordinary
        street.                                                                                       Score 19/25
HOOK 2  (visual-first)                                                                                (SELECTED)
        Picture: an eerily quiet street; he walks alone; a giant dark screen across the road stutters on with HIS FACE
        and "THE ENTIRE WORLD IS SEARCHING FOR YOU." - a fast push into the screen, then a cut to him looking up.
        Words: "You're the only man alive... and now the entire world is looking for you."
        It works with the sound off (the on-screen text and his face tell the story in under 2 seconds), it connects
        to Part 1 in its first four words, and the impact lands on the frame where the screen comes fully on.
                                                                                                      Score 24/25
HOOK 3  (question)  "What happens after you become the last man on Earth?"
        Clear continuity, but a question is a set-up: it promises the story instead of starting it. Score 18/25

RETENTION
- No introduction: frame 1 is already moving (a tracking shot as he walks); the screen switches on at 1.6 s and is
  fully on at 2.0 s with a musical impact; the key caption THE WORLD IS SEARCHING. lands at the end of the first line.
- Fast cuts through the montage (4.2-10.4 s): presenter, giant screens, a sea of people, a situation room, a lab -
  a new shot every 0.5-2.3 s, with stings, static, glitches and a storm of phone notifications.
- Scale goes up scene by scene so Part 2 feels bigger than Part 1: a street, then a square of thousands, then an
  enormous research facility with a holographic globe, then a timeline of the whole planet.
- The pulse stops dead for the black government car; the facility is slow, vast and cold; the timeline ticks; the
  lights go down; the symbol appears; a glitch cuts everything.

ENDING (the cliffhanger)
"You're the only person who might know why everyone else disappeared." The scientist turns to him, the lights dim, an
unknown symbol fills the wall, he stares at it with no idea what it means - the picture glitches and breaks up, and
PART 3? sits on a near-black frame. Nothing is answered: the symbol is never explained.
