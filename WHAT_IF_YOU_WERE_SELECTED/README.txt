WHAT IF YOU WERE SELECTED?
A 59-second vertical Short, made entirely with code
==================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 59.0 s, H.264 + AAC. A hypothetical dystopian story told in the second person ("you"),
following ONE ordinary young adult. On Selection Day a countdown on a giant screen stops - and the name on it is
yours. Within the hour you are walking a Ministry corridor between guards, your face is on every screen in the country,
and you are standing under the logo of the biggest show on Earth: VANTAGE. The arena doors open on a whole landscape
under a geodesic dome - forest, a river, mountains, camera towers. Then you notice the cameras in the trees, your dot
on a live map, the weather changing on cue, the sky glitching. Behind a hidden door you find the control room - and a
list of contestants approved weeks before the "random" selection, your name among them. One switch, and the truth goes
out on every screen in the city. The film ends on you, looking straight into their camera.

Everything you see and hear is generated by code:
- Visuals: Blender's Python API (bpy) builds and renders every frame: the main character (one consistent model in
  every shot: short brown hair, rust-red jacket over a grey hoodie, dark trousers, white trainers, an animated face),
  a supporting cast (the presenter, two guards, a news presenter, two other contestants, a family), a futuristic city
  square with a 60-metre screen, a stage, broadcast cranes, camera drones and ~9,000 people (instanced), the Ministry
  corridor and logo hall, a news studio, an apartment block where every window shows the broadcast, the arena (terrain
  with mountains, a river, ~24,000 instanced trees, grass, a geodesic dome, observation towers, twelve gates, a
  countdown gantry, a camera hidden in a tree, a hidden door), and the hidden monitoring room. No stock footage,
  downloaded models, textures or templates.
- Screens and graphics: tools/textures.py (Pillow) draws the invented VANTAGE logo, the government crest, the giant
  screen's countdown and name card, the news graphics, the arena map, the classified list, monitor overlays, the
  console and the propaganda billboards. The "live feeds" on the monitors and the ID portrait on the screens are
  rendered by animation.py from its own 3D sets (once, before the first frame).
- Captions: tools/captions.py (Pillow): word-by-word captions plus six large "key" captions.
- Music and sound: numpy synthesis in animation.py --stage audio. No samples or recordings.
- Narration: a synthetic voice (Kokoro-82M, voice "bm_george"), made by tools/voiceover.py.


FILES
-----
  script.txt          the narration with measured start/end times (145 words)
  hooks.txt           three opening hooks (two anti-hooks, one traditional), scores, the chosen hook, the open loop
                      and the choice between the two endings
  storyboard.txt      scene-by-scene storyboard: visuals, character action, camera, lighting, sound, narration,
                      on-screen text, key captions, transitions (generated from scene_timing.json by tools/voiceover.py)
  animation.py        the whole production in one Blender script: sets, characters, animation, cameras, lighting and
                      weather, live feeds, render, audio, edit (titles, LIVE bug, glitches, key captions, captions) and
                      encode. Objects are organised in named collections (Square, City, Crowd, Chars, Extras, Facility,
                      News, Homes, Arena, Dome, Forest, Ctrl, Booth, FX, Rig, Lights) and built by reusable functions
                      (instancer, crowd_figure, tower_block, gate_bunker, obs_tower, dome_cam, drone, screen_mat,
                      text_obj, build_char, pose, walk_along, reach, look_at, camera_shot, ...)
  subtitles.srt       subtitles timed from the synthesised voice (short phrases, max 3 words)
  sound_design.txt    the sound idea, every music and sound cue with its time, and a music guide for re-scoring
  sources.txt         what is invented, the claim table (the narration makes no real-world claims), genre references
  README.txt          this file
  final_video.mp4     the finished Short (narration, captions, music and sound)

  scene_timing.json   the single source of truth for timing (scenes, shots, narration, titles, key captions)
  render_all.sh       resumable full render in 200-frame chunks
  tools/              voiceover.py, captions.py, textures.py, contact.py (look-dev contact sheets)
  audio/              narration.wav, mix_with_voice.wav (final mix), mix_no_voice.wav (music + sound only)
  renders/            preview.mp4 (low-resolution preview), final_video_no_voice.mp4 (no narration or captions,
                      for recording your own voice)
  assets/fonts/       Anton, Old Standard TT, Covered By Your Grace (SIL OFL)


THE SIX SCENES (times follow the measured voice; 24 shots, every one with camera movement)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  0.0-7.0   THE SELECTION          Close-up of the face in the crowd, lit by the screen; the countdown stops; one long
                                   crane move pulls back over thousands of people to the name on the 60 m screen. The
                                   presenter at the lectern; the crowd turns round as a camera drone descends.
                                   YOUR NAME WAS SELECTED.
  7.0-15.85 THE WORLD WATCHES      Walking a dark Ministry corridor between two guards while ceiling cameras turn to
                                   follow; the news studio; an apartment block, every window lit by the broadcast; alone
                                   under the giant VANTAGE logo. LIVE bug on screen. MILLIONS ARE WATCHING.
  15.85-29.3 ENTERING THE ARENA    The tunnel; the doors open; a crane reveal of the whole domed landscape; the
                                   countdown to zero; an aerial sweep over the forest, the river and the towers.
                                   WELCOME TO THE ARENA.
  29.3-39.0 THE REALISATION        A camera hidden in a tree turns to follow; the live map with your dot; the light drops
                                   and fog rolls in on cue; two contestants on a ridge; the sky glitches and the dome's
                                   panels show through. YOU ARE BEING WATCHED.
  39.0-49.15 THE TWIST             A hidden door in the rock; the monitoring room (live feeds, the map, the Ministry
                                   crest above the competition logo); the list of pre-approved contestants with your
                                   name; your face in the monitor light. YOU WERE NEVER SUPPOSED TO FIND THIS. /
                                   THE COMPETITION WAS RIGGED.
  49.15-59.0 THE FINAL PAYOFF      The ALL CHANNELS fader; the list on the giant screen in the night square; a
                                   surveillance camera swings round; you turn and look straight into it; the broadcast
                                   breaks up and cuts to black (the first shot is the same face, so it loops).


HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3), ffmpeg, Python 3 with Pillow and numpy (for tools/textures.py and
tools/captions.py). The voice step needs Python with kokoro-onnx and soundfile, plus kokoro-v1.0.onnx and
voices-v1.0.bin from the kokoro-onnx releases. In this repository's Claude Code cloud sessions, the SessionStart hook
installs all of it and exports $BLENDER, $SHORTS_PY, $KOKORO_PY and $KOKORO_DIR.

  cd WHAT_IF_YOU_WERE_SELECTED
  $SHORTS_PY tools/textures.py                      # screen graphics -> build/tex/ (animation.py also runs it if missing)
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, storyboard.txt
  $SHORTS_PY tools/captions.py                      # caption images (the edit stage also runs it if missing)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 5,190,644,1220,1392,1745
                                                    # look-dev stills (frame numbers; --out to choose a folder)
  $SHORTS_PY tools/contact.py renders/frames sheet.jpg 6      # contact sheet of the stills
  ./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
  ./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/ (resumable)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                  # -> final_video.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice       # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --out renders/preview_frames --output preview.mp4 --bitrate 3500

The first render of any kind also renders the eight "live feed" images and the ID portrait into build/tex/ (about a
minute; the file build/tex/feeds_ok marks them done - delete it to render them again after changing the sets).

To inspect the generated 3D scene in Blender's interface: blender -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality
preview --stills 1 --out build/inspect --save-blend build/scene.blend, then open build/scene.blend. (The saved file holds
every set, the cast, the crowd and forest instancers and all camera rigs; the per-frame animation is applied by the
script's frame handler while rendering, so the saved file shows the scene as it is at frame 1.)

On Windows or macOS, replace $BLENDER with the path to blender.exe or Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender and run
render_all.sh's loop by hand (or simply: blender -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality final).

To edit in CapCut instead: render the frames, run the audio stage, then import renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4 (or the
frames plus audio/mix_no_voice.wav), add audio/narration.wav, and use subtitles.srt for the captions. The six key
captions are listed in scene_timing.json ("keys") and in storyboard.txt.

To retime: edit scene_timing.json, re-run tools/voiceover.py and tools/captions.py, then render, audio and edit.
Re-render only the shots whose 3D content or timing you changed.

LIMITATIONS (and the practical choices made)
---------------------------------------------
- Characters: Python alone cannot reliably produce photoreal humans with skeletal facial rigs, so every person is a
  stylised mannequin built from simple shapes, with a hierarchy of joints (hips, spine, chest, neck, shoulders, elbows,
  hips, knees) posed by code each frame, a procedural walk cycle, two-joint arm reaching (IK) and an animated face
  (eyeballs, lids, brows and mouth shape keys). Proportions are adult (about 1.79 m, seven and a half heads); the look
  is deliberately stylised rather than realistic. The crowd is ~9,000 simpler figures instanced by geometry nodes;
  the people near the main character turn round one by one.
- Rendering: Cycles on 4 CPU cores at 540x960 with 6 samples and denoising, upscaled 2x in the edit (captions are drawn
  at full 1080x1920). Fog is done in the compositor from the mist pass; the light shafts are the compositor's sun beams.
- Motion blur: camera motion blur on the fastest camera moves (the opening pull-back, the arena reveal, the aerial).


QUALITY CHECK (against the brief)
---------------------------------
- First second: an extreme close-up of a face in a crowd, lit by a countdown; "The countdown stops." at 0.3 s; the name
  on the giant screen by 2.5 s. The question "selected for what?" is open from the first line.
- The viewer is the person selected: second person throughout, the name card and the crowd turning to "you".
- One consistent main character: the same model (built once by build_char) in all 19 shots that show them.
- Progression: confused (0-7 s) -> frightened (7-16 s) -> awed (16-29 s) -> wary and afraid (29-41 s) -> shocked and
  angry (41-49 s) -> determined (49-59 s).
- Every shot has camera movement and something moving in the world (the countdown, the crowd, cranes and drones,
  ceiling cameras, the TV flicker, the doors, the gantry countdown, other gates opening, the hidden camera, map dots,
  the weather, the dome glitch, the hidden door, monitors, the fader, the surveillance camera).
- Arena: the reveal is one continuous crane move from inside the tunnel up to ~175 m over the forest, the river, the
  towers and the mountains under the dome.
- Captions: Anton, white with a black outline, active word amber, max 3 words, about 64% down the frame; key captions
  larger and amber with a pop-in. The hypothetical label at the top on a dark plate.
- No violence: no weapons, combat or injuries anywhere; the competition's rules are never shown.
- Sound: trailer score with impacts and risers, every surveillance machine audible, the music cut dead after the last
  word; -14 LUFS.


ACCURACY AND HONESTY
--------------------
- The story is fiction and labelled on screen ("A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO"). The narration makes no real-world claims.
- The world is original: the competition (VANTAGE), its logo, the Ministry of Public Harmony and its crest, the city,
  the arena and every character are invented. It belongs to a long genre of televised-contest fiction (see sources.txt)
  but copies no work's names, designs, costumes, symbols or places; the franchise named in the brief's working title is
  not used anywhere in the video.
- Nobody real is depicted; all people are stylised figures, and the names on the list are invented.
