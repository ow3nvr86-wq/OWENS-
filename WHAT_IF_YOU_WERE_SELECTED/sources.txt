WHAT IF YOU WERE SELECTED? - SOURCES, CLAIMS AND WHAT IS INVENTED
==================================================================

This Short is FICTION: a hypothetical scenario, labelled on screen ("A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO") for its whole first scene.
The narration contains no real-world facts, statistics, dates, names or quotes, so there is nothing in it to verify. This
file instead documents (1) what is invented, (2) the genre it belongs to, with checked references, so the originality of
the world can be judged, and (3) the facts that were considered and cut.

- The country, the city, the Ministry, the government crest and the competition (VANTAGE, its logo and its rules).
- The selected person "ALEX VERRAN", their citizen number, the presenter, the guards, the news presenter, the family on

3. THE GENRE - REFERENCES (checked in two sources each)
-------------------------------------------------------
Stories about a televised contest that a society treats as entertainment are an old science-fiction genre. These works
were checked so that this Short borrows the GENRE, not any one work's designs, names, costumes, logos, symbols or places:

- "The Prize of Peril", short story by Robert Sheckley, The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction, May 1958: a contestant on
  a TV show is hunted for a week; the show is rigged. (Wikipedia, "The Prize of Peril"; Galactic Journey review of Store of
  Infinity, 1960 collection, galacticjourney.org/?p=1041.) Adapted as Das Millionenspiel (1970) and Le Prix du Danger (1983).
- "The Running Man", novel by Stephen King writing as Richard Bachman, Signet, 1982: a televised game show in which
  contestants are chased. (Wikipedia, "The Running Man (novel)"; FictionDB, Richard Bachman, The Running Man.)
- "Battle Royale", novel by Koushun Takami, Ohta Publishing, April 1999. (Wikipedia, "Battle Royale (novel)" and
  "Koushun Takami".)
- "The Truman Show", film directed by Peter Weir, written by Andrew Niccol, 1998: a man's life is secretly a live TV show
  run from a hidden control room. (Roger Ebert's review, rogerebert.com; Jonathan Rosenbaum, jonathanrosenbaum.net, 1998.)
- "The Hunger Games", novel by Suzanne Collins, Scholastic, 14 September 2008. (Wikipedia, "The Hunger Games (novel)";
  Open Library edition records.)

How this Short differs (deliberately): no children, no lottery of districts, no tributes, no reaping, no costumes,
salutes, pins, birds or symbols from any of these works; no combat or weapons at all. The arena is a domed artificial
landscape watched by camera towers; the story is about surveillance and a rigged selection, and it ends with the
contestant exposing the broadcast rather than winning it. The franchise name in the brief's working title is not used
anywhere in the video.


4. FACTS CONSIDERED AND CUT
---------------------------
- Real audience figures for the most-watched broadcasts (for "millions are watching"): cut - mixing real ratings into a
  fictional world would blur fact and fiction, and figures differ between sources.
- Real-world facts about CCTV camera numbers in cities: cut for the same reason; the film is not a documentary.
- The publication history above is used only here, for originality notes; none of it is in the narration.
