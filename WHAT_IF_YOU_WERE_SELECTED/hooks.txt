WHAT IF YOU WERE SELECTED? - OPENING HOOKS, OPEN LOOP AND ENDING
================================================================

Goal for the first 1-2 seconds: drop the viewer into the most interesting moment, make it clear something unusual is
happening to THEM, and leave a question they need answered. No "Have you ever wondered...", no "Today we...".


HOOK 1 - ANTI-HOOK: "THE NAME ON THE SCREEN"                                          (CHOSEN)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Picture:  Frame 1 is an extreme close-up of a face in a crowd, cold blue light flickering over it - the reflection of a
          countdown. The beeping stops. The camera pulls back and rises over thousands of heads to reveal a giant screen:
          one name. Theirs.
Voice:    "The countdown stops. The name on the screen... is yours."
Text:     A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO (top) - then the key caption YOUR NAME WAS SELECTED.
Why:      It starts at the climax of the selection, not before it. The viewer is addressed as the person selected in the
          first sentence. The question "selected for WHAT?" is opened at 0.7 s and not answered until the arena.
Scores (1-5):  stops the scroll 5 | instantly clear 5 | puts the viewer in it 5 | open question 5 | visual payoff 4  = 24/25


HOOK 2 - ANTI-HOOK: "THE BROADCAST CUTS IN"
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Picture:  A living room at night. The TV is showing a cooking programme; it cuts to black, then to a government crest:
          THE SELECTION BEGINS NOW. Every phone in the room lights up at once. Then there is a knock at the door.
Voice:    "When the broadcast cut in, nobody in your street moved. Then someone knocked on your door."
Why:      Strong mystery and a good sound moment, but the viewer has to wait 4-5 s to understand what is happening, and
          the most spectacular image (the city and the crowd) is lost.
Scores:   stops the scroll 4 | instantly clear 3 | puts the viewer in it 4 | open question 5 | visual payoff 3  = 19/25


HOOK 3 - TRADITIONAL: "THE BIGGEST SHOW ON EARTH"
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Picture:  An aerial shot over a futuristic city at dusk, every screen showing the same logo.
Voice:    "Every year, one ordinary person is chosen for the biggest show on Earth. This year... it's you."
Why:      Clear, but it explains the premise before showing anything happen - an introduction, not a moment. It also
          gives the twist ("chosen") away as a rule of the world instead of a shock.
Scores:   stops the scroll 3 | instantly clear 5 | puts the viewer in it 3 | open question 3 | visual payoff 3  = 17/25


CHOSEN: HOOK 1. It is the moment the brief calls the most important ("your name appearing on the giant screen"), it is
pure image + one line of voice, and it loops: the last shot of the film is the same face, close up.


THE OPEN LOOP
-------------
Three questions are opened and paid off in order:
  1. Why were you selected?               opened 0:00 ("the name on the screen... is yours")       paid off 0:46 (your name was
                                                                                                   on a pre-approved list, weeks before)
  2. What is waiting inside the arena?    opened 0:11 ("They call it Vantage...")                  paid off 0:16-0:29 (the reveal) - kept
                                                                                                   mysterious: no rules, no combat shown
  3. Can you uncover the truth?           opened 0:35 ("Someone out there is steering the game.")  paid off 0:49-0:57 (the feeds go out live)
Retention beats: a new location or reveal at least every 2-3 s; key captions at 0:05, 0:14, 0:24, 0:31, 0:41, 0:47; the
arena reveal lands at 0:18-0:23, just as the first-scene curiosity would fade.


THE ENDING - TWO OPTIONS
------------------------
A) The brief's line as a statement: "You thought you were selected to play their game. But you might be the one who ends it."
B) The same idea as a question, straight down the lens: "You were selected to play their game. What if you're the one who
   ends it?"
CHOSEN: B. It keeps the brief's payoff, makes the last line an open question for the comments, and is 0.5 s shorter, which
leaves room for the final stare and the broadcast cutting out before 59 s. The final shot (the face in close-up) matches
the first shot, so the Short loops into "The countdown stops."
