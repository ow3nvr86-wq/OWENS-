THE DAY POMPEII DISAPPEARED - 79 AD
RESEARCH, VERIFIED TIMELINE, SOURCES AND HONESTY NOTES
============================================================

Written before production. Every date, number and name used in the narration or on screen is listed in
section 4 with the sources that support it. Where sources disagree, the video uses the cautious figure or says
"about" / "an estimated". Nothing in the video is presented as a quote from a Roman resident, and no ancient or
modern document is invented.


1. SOURCES
----------
Ancient eyewitness
[PLINY]  Pliny the Younger, Letters 6.16 and 6.20, written to the historian Tacitus about 25 years after the
         eruption. The only surviving eyewitness account. He watched from Misenum, across the Bay of Naples; his
         uncle, Pliny the Elder, commander of the fleet there, sailed toward the eruption and died at Stabiae.
         He compared the eruption cloud to a pine tree (an umbrella pine), and described the darkness, falling
         ash and pumice, and flashes in the cloud. Translations hosted by the University of Virginia
         (https://pompeii.iath.virginia.edu/pliny.html) and summarised by the University of Rhode Island
         (https://volcano.uri.edu/vesuvius/Plinys/Plinys.html) and the US Naval Academy library
         (https://usna.edu/Library/sca/blog/posts/vesuvius_i.php).

Volcanology
[SIG]    H. Sigurdsson, S. Carey, W. Cornell, T. Pescatore (1985), "The eruption of Vesuvius in A.D. 79",
         National Geographic Research 1; and Sigurdsson, Cashdollar & Sparks (1982), stratigraphy of the 79 AD
         deposits. The standard reconstruction: a Plinian phase of white, then grey, pumice fall, followed on the
         second day by pyroclastic surges and flows; summarised by Michigan Tech
         (https://geo.mtu.edu/volcanoes/boris/mirror/mirrored_html/VESUVIO_79.html) and the University of Rhode
         Island (https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/gsofacpubs/1114).
[CHIO]   Chiominto, doctoral thesis, University of Naples Federico II (2023), on the fall phases of the 79 AD
         eruption: about 6 h 36 min of white pumice fall and about 11 h of grey pumice fall
         (https://www.fedoa.unina.it/15239/).
[UNINA]  University of Naples Federico II and Pompeii Archaeological Park, field studies of the deposits inside
         Pompeii: the basal pumice-fall deposit is 0 to 4.5 m thick across the site, the overlying ash-flow
         (current) deposits a few tens of cm to 2 m; "almost six metres" of pyroclastic material in total
         (https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/849238, https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-9665.html).
[WIKI-E] "Eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD", Wikipedia (citing Sigurdsson et al. and others): eruption
         column up to about 33 km; first Plinian phase 18 to 20 hours; the surges that destroyed Pompeii came in
         the early morning of the second day; at Pompeii about 2.8 m of pumice fall and 1.8 m of surge deposits;
         by 2003 about 1,044 casts and remains of about 100 more people recovered in and around Pompeii; total
         death toll unknown. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruption_of_Mount_Vesuvius_in_79_AD

Archaeology and history
[PARK]   Parco Archeologico di Pompei (Pompeii Archaeological Park), official fact sheet and press kit: the
         ancient city covers about 66 hectares, about two-thirds (about 44 ha) excavated, about 1,500 buildings
         brought to light (https://pompeiisites.org/wp-content/uploads/Scheda_ParcoArcheologicoDiPompei.pdf,
         https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-park-of-pompeii/press-kit).
[GAL]    Museo Galileo, "The eruption of Vesuvius and the history of the archaeological excavations": digging at
         the site called "Civita" began in 1748 under the Bourbon king Charles; engineer Roque Joaquin de Alcubierre
         (https://mostre.museogalileo.it/homofaber/en/introduction.html).
[HT]     History Today, "Pompeii is Found": the site was identified as Pompeii in August 1763 from an inscription
         naming the "res publica Pompeianorum" (the cippus of T. Suedius Clemens)
         (https://www.historytoday.com/archive/months-past/pompeii-found).
[CSCP]   Cambridge School Classics Project, "Pompeii - AD 79; 1748-1860" (https://www.na4.cambridgescp.com/weblink/449).
[FIOR]   Giuseppe Fiorelli, director of the excavations from the 1860s, introduced the method of pouring plaster
         into the cavities left in the ash by victims' bodies (The Florentine, https://www.theflorentine.net/2022/09/14/giuseppe-fiorelli/).
[62AD]   "AD 62 Pompeii earthquake", Wikipedia; University of Virginia, "Patterns of Reconstruction at Pompeii"
         (https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/struct/pompeii/patterns/sec-02.html): repairs after the earthquake of
         62/63 AD were still under way in many buildings in 79 AD.

The date debate
[DATE]   CNN (2018), "Pompeii eruption inscription date" (the charcoal inscription "XVI K NOV" = 17 October);
         National Geographic, "Mount Vesuvius eruption"; History.com, "Pompeii destruction date"; Finestre
         sull'Arte (2024), "Zuchtriegel reopens debate on Pompeii's eruption: August or October"
         (https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/pompeii-eruption-inscription-date-intl,
         https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/mount-vesuvius-eruption-pompeii,
         https://www.history.com/articles/pompeii-vesuvius-destruction-date,
         https://finestresullarte.info/en/news-focus/zuchtriegel-surprisingly-reopens-debate-on-pompeii-s-eruption-august-or-october).


2. VERIFIED TIMELINE
--------------------
62/63 AD     A strong earthquake damages Pompeii and the towns around the bay. Repairs are still under way in many
             buildings when Vesuvius erupts 16-17 years later. [62AD]
79 AD        The eruption. Traditional date 24-25 August, from manuscripts of Pliny's letter. Many scholars now
             argue for late October (autumn fruit, clothing, the 2018 charcoal inscription); in 2024 the park
             showed that a charcoal note can survive for months, so the inscription could date to 78 AD and the
             August date cannot be ruled out. The video says only "79 AD". [DATE]
Day 1, from  Plinian phase. Around midday or early afternoon (Pliny: about the seventh hour) a column of gas, ash
about midday and pumice rises, at its peak an estimated ~30 km (up to about 33 km), and spreads into an umbrella
             cloud that the wind carries south-east over Pompeii. White pumice falls for roughly 6-7 hours, then
             grey pumice, about 18 hours of fall in all. Roofs collapse under the weight; the sun is blotted out.
             [PLINY, SIG, CHIO, WIKI-E]
Night and    The column partly collapses several times, sending pyroclastic density currents (surges and flows:
day 2        fast, ground-hugging avalanches of hot gas, ash and rock) down the volcano's flanks. Herculaneum is
             overwhelmed during the night. [SIG, WIKI-E]
Day 2, early The surges reach Pompeii and sweep through the town in the early morning, killing those still there
morning      and burying it further. [SIG, WIKI-E]
Result       Pompeii lies under several metres of pumice and ash: "almost six metres" in total, varying from place to
             place. [UNINA, WIKI-E]
1590s        Domenico Fontana's water channel from the Sarno is cut through the buried city; the ruins are not
             recognised. [HT, CSCP]
1748         Excavation begins at "Civita" under Charles of Bourbon. [GAL, CSCP]
1763         An inscription confirms the site is Pompeii. [HT]
1860s        Giuseppe Fiorelli directs the excavations and makes the first plaster casts from cavities in the ash.
             [FIOR]
Today        About 44 of the city's ~66 hectares have been excavated; about a third is still buried, and digging
             continues. [PARK]

5. CONFIRMED vs. UNCERTAIN vs. NOT CLAIMED
------------------------------------------
CONFIRMED: the eruption in 79 AD; the sequence of a long pumice fall followed by pyroclastic density currents;
Pompeii buried by several metres of deposits; the 1748 start of excavation; the 1763 identification; about
two-thirds of the city excavated today.

UNCERTAIN (and how the video handles it):
- The month (August or October). The video says "79 AD" and flags the debate on screen.
- Exact clock times. Only Pliny gives a time for the start ("about the seventh hour"); reconstructions of the
  later phases differ by hours. The video says "for hours" and "next morning" and gives no clock times.
- The height of the column (estimates from ~26 to ~33 km). The video says "an estimated thirty kilometres".
- Pompeii's population (estimates range from ~10,000 to ~20,000 or more) and the death toll (unknown). The video
  gives NO numbers for either, as the brief asked.
- What the eruption looked like from inside the town: no images exist. Every scene is an artistic
  reconstruction based on the deposits and on Pliny's description, and is labelled as one.

NOT CLAIMED: no invented conversations, documents or quotes; no named individual residents; no depiction of the
death of any person (the pyroclastic currents are shown at landscape scale, sweeping over the empty town); no
plaster casts or human remains are shown.


6. WHAT IS ILLUSTRATIVE (ARTISTIC RECONSTRUCTION, NOT RECORD)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- The town plan is a simplified, schematic Pompeii: a grid of city blocks, a walled oval, a forum with a temple
  on a podium at its north end with Vesuvius behind it (as at the real Temple of Jupiter), streets with raised
  pavements, stepping stones and cart ruts. It is not a survey of the real streets.
- The shape and height of Vesuvius before 79 AD are not known exactly; it is shown as a single broad cone.
- The eruption column is drawn far shorter than ~30 km so it fits in a vertical frame; the umbrella cloud,
  the darkening and the flashes in the cloud follow Pliny's description.
- Pyroclastic currents are shown as a ground-hugging, billowing ash cloud; their real speed (often tens of m/s)
  is compressed for the edit.
- The people are stylised figures in plausible Roman clothing (tunics, stolas, pallas, cloaks); none represents
  a real person. The excavation workers are generic, not a specific dig.
- The passage of time (vegetation over the deposits, day and night) is a time-lapse device.


7. FACTS CHECKED BUT CUT FOR TIME
---------------------------------
- Pliny the Elder's death at Stabiae while trying to reach friends by sea.
- Herculaneum, buried far deeper (about 20 m) and preserved differently (carbonised wood, skeletons in the boat
  houses).
- The 62/63 AD earthquake and the repairs still under way in 79 AD.
- Fontana's 1590s water channel that ran through the buried town unrecognised.
- Fiorelli's plaster casts (1860s); deliberately not shown, out of respect.
- The unexploded Second World War bombs and the 1943 Allied bombing of the site.
