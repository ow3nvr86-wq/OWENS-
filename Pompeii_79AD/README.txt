THE DAY POMPEII DISAPPEARED - 79 AD
A 60-second vertical historical documentary Short, made entirely with code
============================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 60 s, H.264 + AAC. A dark, cinematic reconstruction of the eruption of Mount
Vesuvius in 79 AD: life in Pompeii, the eruption column, the long fall of pumice, the darkness, the pyroclastic
density currents of the next morning, the town buried under several metres of deposits, the centuries in
which it lay hidden, the excavation that began in 1748, and the ruined forum today. It ends on the line: "The
eruption that buried Pompeii also preserved it."

Everything you see and hear is generated by code:
- Visuals: Blender's Python API (bpy) builds and renders every frame: landscape, Vesuvius, the town, streets,
  buildings, people, effects, lights and cameras. No stock footage, no downloaded model or texture, no template.
- Music and sound: numpy synthesis in animation.py --stage audio. No samples or recordings.
- Narration: a synthetic voice (Kokoro-82M, voice "bm_george"), made by tools/voiceover.py.


FILES
-----
  script.txt          narration, scene by scene, with start and end times (125 words)
  storyboard.txt      the 11 scenes: visuals, shots and camera moves, narration, on-screen text, events, edit
                      notes (generated from scene_timing.json)
  asset_plan.txt      environments, characters, objects, materials and effects, and which function builds each
  animation.py        the whole production in one Blender script: 3D scene, animation, render, audio, edit, encode
  subtitles.srt       subtitles timed from the synthesised voice
  sound_design.txt    every music and sound cue with its time, plus suggested assets for re-scoring
  sources.txt         research: sources, verified timeline, claim-by-claim table, confirmed vs uncertain, what is
                      illustrative, facts cut for time
  README.txt          this file
  final_video.mp4     the finished Short (narration, captions, music and sound)
  pompeii.blend       the generated Blender project (scene, materials, cameras, keyframes); see "THE .BLEND FILE"

  scene_timing.json   the single source of truth for timing (scenes, shots, narration slots, titles, events)
  render_all.sh       resumable full render in 200-frame chunks
  tools/voiceover.py  narration synthesis; writes script.txt, subtitles.srt, storyboard.txt, build/vo_timing.json
  tools/contact.py    contact sheets of look-dev stills
  audio/              narration.wav, mix_with_voice.wav (final mix), mix_no_voice.wav (music + sound only)
  renders/            preview.mp4 (low-resolution preview), final_video_no_voice.mp4 (no narration or captions,
                      for recording your own voice in CapCut)
  assets/fonts/       Anton, Old Standard TT, Courier Prime, Covered By Your Grace (SIL OFL), Special Elite (Apache 2.0)


THE ELEVEN SCENES
-----------------
 0-4    THE MOUNTAIN         A fast push over the rooftops toward Vesuvius as the column bursts from its summit.
 4-10   LIFE IN POMPEII      Eye level up a busy street: shops, a tavern counter, a stall, a handcart, people.
 10-15  THE FIRST WARNING    In the forum a group turns to the mountain; the camera tilts up to the column.
 15-21  THE SKY CHANGES      Low angle past the temple to the spreading cloud; pumice on roof tiles; the town.
 21-27  THE STREETS FILL     The street under falling pumice; people hurry through with cloths and torches.
 27-33  INTENSIFIES          The town in near darkness beneath the cloud; flashes inside it.
 33-39  THE CURRENTS         The column collapses; a billowing current pours down the slopes and over the town.
 39-44  POMPEII DISAPPEARS   Overhead: the town sinks under grey deposits; only some wall tops break through.
 44-50  CENTURIES PASS       Time-lapse: days and nights, clouds, grass and trees; AD 79 -> AD 1748.
 50-56  REDISCOVERY          A crane down into the excavation: the street found again, workers, the layers.
 56-60  THE FINAL SHOT       The ruined forum today with Vesuvius beyond (echoing the opening); final caption.


HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3) and ffmpeg. The voice step needs Python with kokoro-onnx and
soundfile, plus kokoro-v1.0.onnx and voices-v1.0.bin from the kokoro-onnx releases. In this repository's Claude
Code cloud sessions, the SessionStart hook installs all of it and exports $BLENDER, $SHORTS_PY, $KOKORO_PY and
$KOKORO_DIR.

  cd Pompeii_79AD
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, storyboard.txt
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 100,210,420,500,560,615,760,900,1120,1260,1420,1620,1760
                                                    # look-dev stills (one per shot) -> renders/frames (use --out)
  ./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
  ./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/ (resumable)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                  # -> final_video.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice       # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --out renders/preview_frames --output preview.mp4 --bitrate 3500
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stills 1 --out build/x --save-blend pompeii.blend   # the project file

On Windows or macOS, replace $BLENDER with the path to blender.exe or Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender and run
render_all.sh's loop by hand (or just: blender -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality final).

Render budget on 4 CPU cores with no GPU (Cycles, OIDN denoising):
  final   540x960, 6 samples, upscaled 2x in the edit   about 17 s per frame here: about 8.5 hours for 1,782 frames
  preview 270x480, 4 samples                            roughly 2-4 s per frame, about 1.5 hours
  Building the scene takes about 20 s. A desktop GPU renders this many times faster (set the Cycles device to GPU).
  The brief suggested EEVEE: on this headless, GPU-less machine EEVEE has no OpenGL context and software GL is
  slower than Cycles, so Cycles is used; on a desktop with a GPU, EEVEE Next can render the same scene.

To retime: edit scene_timing.json, re-run tools/voiceover.py, then the audio and edit stages. Re-render 3D frames
only if a shot boundary moves or anything in the 3D scene changes.

To record your own voice in CapCut: import renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4 and read script.txt, using the start
times in script.txt or subtitles.srt.


THE .BLEND FILE
---------------
pompeii.blend is saved by animation.py --save-blend: the whole generated scene with its materials, cameras (one
per shot, switched by timeline markers) and keyframed camera moves (saved compressed, about 27 MB). The per-frame animation (people, the eruption
cloud, particles, deposits, light changes) is computed by the frame handler in animation.py, which a saved .blend
cannot carry; to render the full animation, run animation.py (it rebuilds the scene in about 20 seconds).


ACCURACY
--------
- Dates, numbers and names are checked against at least two sources (sources.txt, section 4).
- Confirmed: the eruption in 79 AD; a long pumice fall followed by pyroclastic density currents the next morning;
  Pompeii buried by several metres of deposits (up to about 6 m); excavation from 1748; about a third of the
  city still unexcavated.
- Uncertain, and handled carefully: the month (August or October; the video says only "79 AD" and flags the
  debate on screen); the column height (said as "an estimated thirty kilometres"); no clock times; no population
  or death-toll figures.
- Artistic reconstruction, labelled on screen: the town plan is schematic; the shape of Vesuvius before 79 AD is
  not known exactly; the column is drawn about 9.5 km tall to fit the vertical frame; the pumice fall, the
  burial and the currents are compressed in time; the flashes follow Pliny the Younger's description.
- No individual death is depicted; the people are stylised figures, not portraits; no plaster casts or remains
  are shown. No conversations, documents or quotes are invented.
