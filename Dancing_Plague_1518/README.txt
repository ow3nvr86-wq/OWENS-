THE DANCING PLAGUE OF 1518 - STRASBOURG'S STRANGEST MYSTERY
A 60-second vertical dark-history Short in 2D illustrated animation, made entirely with code
============================================================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 59.6 s, H.264 + AAC. In July 1518 a woman began dancing in a Strasbourg
street and, it seems, could not stop. Within a week dozens had joined her. The city's physicians blamed
"hot blood", and the council's cure was more dancing - halls, a stage, hired musicians - until it banned
public dancing and sent the dancers to a shrine of Saint Vitus. The video ends on the open question:
historians still debate what really happened.

2D ONLY. There is no 3D anywhere: no Blender, no 3D models, no 3D renderer or camera. Every frame is
drawn with pycairo as flat vector shapes with an ink outline, cel shadows and engraving hatches; numpy
adds paper, grain, fog and the colour grade; PIL draws the captions; ffmpeg encodes.

THE 2D TECHNIQUES
-----------------
- Character animation: a 2D puppet rig per character (body, limbs, hands, broad-toed shoes, skirts, coats,
  headwear, faces with eyes, lids, brows, nose and mouth) driven by pose functions: a walk cycle, five
  dance cycles with their own rhythms (whirl, jig, convulse, stomp, sway, plus a full-turn twirl drawn by
  flipping the drawing), idles with breathing and blinking, head turns, reactions, gestures and
  musicians playing. Characters are animated on twos (a new drawing every second frame, 15 a second),
  like hand-drawn animation, and every new drawing is retraced slightly differently (line boil).
  Skirts swing and flare and ride up over a lifted knee; no two dancers share a phase.
- Backgrounds: each set is painted once into separate layers (sky, distant skyline, far buildings, near
  buildings, foreground). A multiplane camera moves and scales each layer by a different amount, which
  gives depth without any 3D. The street is drawn in one-point perspective like a period engraving.
- Environment: drifting fog banks, chimney smoke, floating dust, flickering candles whose light breathes
  across the room, ripples and a boat on the river, birds, rain, a lightning flash.
- Transitions: moving foreground object (a timber post), ink spread, paper tear, page turns, an
  animated map transition (the stage shrinks into the city plan), cross dissolves, and a match cut back
  to the opening street.
- Finishing: an aged-paper texture multiplied over every frame (shifted every second frame so the paper
  itself 'boils'), a palette grade that darkens scene by scene, grain and vignette.


HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Requirements: Python 3 with numpy, scipy, pillow and pycairo; ffmpeg. The voice needs kokoro-onnx with
kokoro-v1.0.onnx and voices-v1.0.bin. In this repository's cloud sessions the SessionStart hook installs
the shared tools and exports $SHORTS_PY and $KOKORO_PY (pycairo: $SHORTS_PY -m pip install pycairo).

  cd Dancing_Plague_1518
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py              # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, build/vo_timing.json
  $SHORTS_PY src/sets.py && $SHORTS_PY src/sets2.py        # draw the background layers (about 1.5 min)
  $SHORTS_PY -c "import sys; sys.path.insert(0,'src'); import post; post.save_paper()"
  $SHORTS_PY audio.py                        # music, ambience, effects, mixes, sound_design.txt
  $SHORTS_PY render.py --stills 0.5,3,12,20  # look-dev stills -> build/stills/
  $SHORTS_PY render.py --preview             # -> renders/preview.mp4
  $SHORTS_PY render.py                       # -> final_video.mp4
  $SHORTS_PY render.py --no-voice            # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4
  $SHORTS_PY tools/export_assets.py          # character and prop sheets

Render time on 4 CPU cores: preview about 0.25-0.6 s per frame per core (about 4 minutes); final
1080x1920 about 1-2.5 s per frame per core (about 15 minutes). Four worker processes render in parallel.


ACCURACY
--------
- Facts are checked in sources.txt (section 4). July 1518, Strasbourg, one woman first, dozens within a
  week, the physicians' "hot blood" verdict, halls, a stage and musicians, the ban, the shrine of Saint
  Vitus at Saverne, and the end by early September are well supported.
- Uncertain, and said with care: how many took part ("some accounts say hundreds"; on screen "some
  accounts: up to 400"); the first dancer's name (not used); deaths (not mentioned or shown, because the
  contemporary record is unclear); the cause (each theory is labelled as a belief or a modern theory, and
  ergot is labelled disputed).
- Artistic reconstruction, labelled on screen: every person, face, costume, street, room and the plan of
  the city is invented in the general style of the Upper Rhine about 1500-1520; no dialogue or document
  text is shown; the shrine's look is imagined.
