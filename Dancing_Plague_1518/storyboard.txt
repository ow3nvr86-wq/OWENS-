THE DANCING PLAGUE OF 1518 - SCENE-BY-SCENE STORYBOARD
======================================================

59.6 s, 1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps. 2D only: every frame is drawn with pycairo vector ink (render.py, src/).
Characters are animated on twos (a new drawing every second frame) with line boil; the camera, fog, dust,
candles and water move on every frame. Depth comes only from flat layers moved at different speeds (a
multiplane camera). Timing comes from scene_timing.json and the measured narration (build/vo_timing.json).

Palette: dark charcoal, muted brown, aged parchment, dusty grey, deep burgundy, desaturated green and warm
candlelight. The grade darkens scene by scene (look "dark" 0.0 -> 0.6), with colder tints near the end.
Captions: bold Cinzel capitals in parchment with a dark outline; key words on a burgundy ink plate; max
3 words per chunk at 65% of the frame height. Titles at the top, clear of the Shorts interface.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SCENE 1 - THE FIRST DANCER                                   0.0 - 6.0 s   (frames 1-180)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual    A narrow street of jettied half-timbered houses, cobbles with a central gutter, trade signs (a
          baker's pretzel), the cathedral spire at the far end. A woman in a burgundy gown, linen apron and
          white coif dances in the middle of the street, arms raised, head thrown back, eyes half closed.
Camera    Opens tight on her broad-toed shoes and swirling hem on the cobbles (zoom 4.3, high-resolution
          cobble patch), tilts up her body to her face (2.6 s), then pulls back (to 5 s) to reveal the
          onlookers framing her and the whole street. The layers separate in parallax as the camera widens.
Animation 'Whirl' dance cycle (1.35 s): alternate knee lifts, swaying hips, skirt flare and swirl, arms
          overhead. Ten onlookers turn their heads to her from 2.6 s, staggered (stare, point, hand to
          mouth, recoil, crossing herself, whispering); a man crossing the street stops and turns. Chimney
          smoke, drifting fog.
Narration 0.15-5.90  "July, 1518. A woman began dancing in a Strasbourg street... and seemingly couldn't stop."
Text      Tag: AN ILLUSTRATED RECONSTRUCTION
Sound     A lone pipe starts the dance tune on the first frame; her shoe taps; a soft tabor; a murmur rises.
Out       MOVING FOREGROUND WIPE (5.58-6.42): a dark timber post sweeps across the lens; the cut hides behind it.

SCENE 2 - THE CITY                                           6.0 - 10.0 s  (frames 181-300)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual    Strasbourg on the river Ill: the cathedral's west front and single spire above the roofs of the old
          island, tall roofs with rows of dormers, gabled half-timbered houses on the water, a footbridge,
          a market quay with striped stalls, barrels and goods.
Camera    A slow sideways truck to the right. Six layers move at different speeds: sky 0.1, roofs and
          cathedral 0.3, riverside houses 0.6, quay 1.0, foreground awning and hanging herbs 1.35.
Animation Townspeople walk both ways on the quay (walk cycles, varied speeds); a stallholder and customer
          haggle; a boatman poles a flat boat; ripples on the water; birds; chimney smoke; fog banks.
Narration 6.30-9.89  "A crowded city, already worn down by hunger and disease."
Text      STRASBOURG (blackletter) / JULY 1518
Sound     Three distant cathedral bells, market murmur, footsteps; the tune far off.
Out       INK SPREAD (9.6-10.4): ink blooms from the centre and floods the page.

SCENE 3 - IT SPREADS                                         10.0 - 15.5 s (frames 301-465)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual    The same street. Three beats: one dancer -> several dancers -> a crowded street.
Camera    Starts close on her (zoom 1.55) and pulls back to the full street; a slight uneasy roll.
Animation Each new dancer appears through an ink-bloom reveal (a spreading blot with splatter): one man at
          11.0 s, five more from 12.6 s, sixteen more from 13.6 s. Five dance cycles (jig, convulse,
          stomp, sway, a full-turn twirl drawn by flipping the drawing each half turn), each with its own
          tempo and phase, so no two move alike. Onlookers press against the walls.
Narration 10.30-14.64  "Within a week, dozens had joined her. Some accounts say hundreds, by August."
Text      DOZENS WITHIN A WEEK -> SOME ACCOUNTS: UP TO 400
Sound     The tune grows (pipe + shawm + tabor); stamping feet multiply; crowd; a low drone enters.
Out       PAPER TEAR (15.12-15.88): the page tears along a ragged diagonal and falls away.

SCENE 4 - THE CITY PANICS                                    15.5 - 21.5 s (frames 466-645)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual    A candlelit council chamber: beamed ceiling, tall leaded windows, carved panelling, the city's arms
          (argent, a bend gules). Councillors in fur-trimmed gowns and flat caps behind a long cloth-covered
          table with parchments, an inkwell, books and candles. Two physicians in long robes and round caps:
          one holds up a urine flask to the light, the other reads from a book. A clerk writes.
Camera    A slow pan from left to right across the room (zoom 1.32).
Animation Talking hand gestures, a hand to the chin, folded arms, writing hand, the flask raised; blinking;
          flickering candles whose light breathes over the whole room; a paper lifting in a draught; dust.
Narration 15.80-21.04  "The council turned to physicians. Their verdict: a natural illness, caused by hot blood."
Text      THE PHYSICIANS' VERDICT / "HOT BLOOD"
Sound     A low drone; the tune muffled through the walls; quill scratch; paper; candle crackle.
Out       PAGE TURN (21.05-21.95): an illustrated page turns right to left, its parchment back crossing over.

SCENE 5 - THE STRANGE SOLUTION                               21.5 - 28.0 s (frames 646-840)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual    The market square: the great roof of the grain hall, houses, a wooden stage with steps. On the
          stage, exhausted dancers; beside them three hired musicians (tabor drum, shawm, bagpipe). Behind,
          a row of watching townspeople; in front, two officials with folded arms behind a rope barrier.
Camera    A slow push in with a creeping roll (-0.7 to +1 degree): unsettling.
Animation Musicians play in time (drum strikes, fingering, bagpipe pumping); dancers in convulse, sway,
          stomp and jig cycles, with tiredness drooping their heads; the crowd reacts.
Narration 21.80-26.96  "Their cure? More dancing. Halls were cleared, a stage was built, and musicians were hired."
Text      THE "CURE" / GUILD HALLS . A WOODEN STAGE . HIRED MUSICIANS
Sound     The hired band, too loud, one shawm slowly sliding out of tune; stamping; crowd.
Out       ANIMATED MAP TRANSITION (27.5-28.5): the stage picture shrinks, framed in ink, into its place on
          the city plan.

SCENE 6 - THE MYSTERY DEEPENS                                28.0 - 34.5 s (frames 841-1035)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual    A bird's-eye plan of the city in the manner of a 16th-century town view: the Ill round the old
          island, walls and towers, bridges, blocks of roofs, the cathedral, the market, a compass rose.
Camera    From close on the market (zoom 2.3) out to the whole plan, then a slow push.
Animation Groups of tiny dancers appear in twelve streets one after another, each with a burgundy glow and
          ink ripples; they circle and flail. The page darkens towards the edges.
Narration 28.30-32.01  "It only seemed to spread. So the council banned public dancing."
Text      PUBLIC DANCING AND MUSIC BANNED; ink stamp (31.9 s): WHY WERE THEY DANCING?
Sound     The band plays on until "banned" (30.6 s), then stops dead; silence, a drone, a heartbeat; the
          stamp lands with a deep hit.
Out       INK SPREAD (34.1-34.9).

SCENE 7 - THE THEORIES                                       34.5 - 43.0 s (frames 1036-1290)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three illustrated plates on dark parchment with an engraved border, each pushed in slowly.
7a 34.5-37.6  BELIEF AT THE TIME / A SAINT'S CURSE: a statue of the young Saint Vitus in a niche, with a
          halo, his martyr's palm and his cockerel; candles flicker before him; a dancer jerks in the
          foreground in silhouette; rays of light.
7b 37.6-40.3  MODERN THEORY / MASS PSYCHOGENIC ILLNESS: rows of townspeople; a wave spreads from one person

7c 40.3-43.0  MODERN THEORY . DISPUTED / ERGOT POISONING: ears of rye drawn like a botanical engraving, some
          grains replaced by the dark ergot sclerotia; a magnifying lens passes over them; a loaf and sickle.
Joined by PAGE TURNS at 37.6 and 40.3.
Narration 34.80-41.89  "Many feared a curse of Saint Vitus. Modern theories range from mass psychogenic
          illness... to toxic, mouldy grain."
Sound     Choir and a small bell; a quickening heartbeat with whispers; a thin wavering tone over a drone.
Out       CROSS DISSOLVE (42.5-43.5).

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Animation A procession walks the road led by a priest with a cross: tired walk cycles, two still dancing as

Narration 43.30-48.63  "In the end, the dancers were taken to a shrine of Saint Vitus, in the hills, to pray for healing."

Out       MATCH CUT (50.0): back to the street of the opening, in the same framing.

SCENE 9 - THE FINAL MYSTERY                                  50.0 - 59.6 s (frames 1501-1788)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual    The same street at dusk, empty, colder and darker, fog in the lane, dust drifting.
Camera    A slow push down the street (50.0-52.6), then a tilt down onto the empty cobbles where she danced
          (to 54.4) - the exact framing of the first shot, without her.
Then      CROSS DISSOLVE (54.3-55.3) into dark parchment, where her figure dances on faintly, like an old
          engraving in the paper. Ink stamp (55.0): WHAT REALLY HAPPENED IN STRASBOURG?
          Tag (57.0): HISTORIANS STILL DEBATE THE CAUSE. Fade to black 59.2-59.6.
Narration 50.90-57.38  "By September, the dancing had faded. Five centuries later, historians still debate
          what really happened."
Sound     Wind, one distant bell, a low drone; the stamp; the dance tune returns faint and alone, and fades.
Loop      The last live image is the empty cobbles of the first frame: replayed, the Short loops back to her
          feet on the same stones.
