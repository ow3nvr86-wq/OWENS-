WHAT IF MONEY GREW ON TREES?
A 58.5-second vertical comedy-documentary Short, made entirely with code
=========================================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 58.5 s, H.264 + AAC. A completely serious documentary narrator describes the most
ridiculous economic disaster in history: banknotes start growing on trees, everyone becomes a millionaire, farmers
swap corn for cash, a man buys a whole forest... and a sandwich ends up costing $9,999,999.99. It ends with the real
lesson - more money chasing the same amount of goods pushes prices up - and a last joke: "Turns out, money really
doesn't grow on trees... Well, technically, now it does." The last shot re-creates the first, so the Short loops.

Everything you see and hear is generated by code:
- Visuals: Blender's Python API (bpy) builds and renders every frame: a supermarket, a suburban street with the money
  tree, a farm and money orchard, a forest of money trees and a city street with a giant screen, plus a cast of
  reusable stylised characters with animated faces. No stock footage, downloaded models, textures or templates.
- Printed props: tools/textures.py (Pillow/numpy) draws the banknote (an invented design), price tags, the sandwich
  card, banners, product labels, signs and the screen's graph background.
- Captions: tools/captions.py (Pillow) draws the word-by-word captions.
- Music and sound: numpy synthesis in animation.py --stage audio. No samples or recordings.
- Narration: a synthetic voice (Kokoro-82M, voice "bm_george"), made by tools/voiceover.py.


FILES
-----
  script.txt          the narration with measured start/end times (137 caption words; 143 words as spoken)
  hooks.txt           the three opening-hook options, how they scored, and why option 1 was chosen
  storyboard.txt      scene-by-scene storyboard: visuals, characters, camera, lighting, sound, narration, on-screen
                      text, events, transitions (generated from scene_timing.json by tools/voiceover.py)
  animation.py        the whole production in one Blender script: sets, characters, animation, cameras, render,
                      audio, edit and encode
  subtitles.srt       subtitles timed from the synthesised voice (short phrases, max 3 words)
  sound_design.txt    every music and sound cue with its time, plus a music guide for re-scoring
  sources.txt         sources, claim table, confirmed vs disputed vs speculation, what is illustrative
  README.txt          this file
  final_video.mp4     the finished Short (narration, captions, music and sound)

  scene_timing.json   the single source of truth for timing (scenes, shots, narration slots, titles, events)
  render_all.sh       resumable full render in 200-frame chunks
  tools/voiceover.py  narration synthesis; writes script.txt, subtitles.srt, storyboard.txt, build/vo_timing.json
                      (word timings are snapped to the real pauses in the synthesised voice)
  tools/textures.py   printed props (written to build/tex/)
  tools/captions.py   word-by-word caption images (written to build/captions/)
  tools/contact.py    contact sheets of look-dev stills
  audio/              narration.wav, mix_with_voice.wav (final mix), mix_no_voice.wav (music + sound only)
  renders/            preview.mp4 (low-resolution preview), final_video_no_voice.mp4 (no narration or captions,
                      for recording your own voice)
  assets/fonts/       Anton, Old Standard TT, Covered By Your Grace (SIL OFL)

HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3), ffmpeg, Python 3 with Pillow and numpy (for tools/textures.py and
tools/captions.py). The voice step needs Python with kokoro-onnx and soundfile, plus kokoro-v1.0.onnx and
voices-v1.0.bin from the kokoro-onnx releases. In this repository's Claude Code cloud sessions, the SessionStart hook
installs all of it and exports $BLENDER, $SHORTS_PY, $KOKORO_PY and $KOKORO_DIR.

  cd WHAT_IF_MONEY_GREW_ON_TREES
  $SHORTS_PY tools/textures.py                      # printed props -> build/tex/ (animation.py also runs it if missing)
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, storyboard.txt
  $SHORTS_PY tools/captions.py                      # caption images (the edit stage also runs it if missing)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 46,121,160,208,280,355
                                                    # look-dev stills (frame numbers; --out to choose a folder)
  ./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
  ./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/ (resumable)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                  # -> final_video.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice       # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --out renders/preview_frames --output preview.mp4 --bitrate 3500

On Windows or macOS, replace $BLENDER with the path to blender.exe or Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender and run
render_all.sh's loop by hand (or simply: blender -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality final).

Render budget on 4 CPU cores with no GPU (Cycles, OIDN denoising):
  final   540x960, 6 samples, upscaled 2x in the edit   about 7-9 s per frame -> about 4-4.5 hours for 1,755 frames
  preview 270x480, 4 samples                            about 2 s per frame   -> about 1 hour
  audio stage about 20 s; edit stage about 10-15 minutes. A desktop GPU renders all of this many times faster.

To retime: edit scene_timing.json, re-run tools/voiceover.py and tools/captions.py, then the audio and edit stages.
Re-render 3D frames only if you move a shot boundary or change anything in the 3D scene.

To record your own voice: import renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4 into an editor (e.g. CapCut) and read script.txt,
using the start times in script.txt or subtitles.srt.


ACCURACY
--------
- The scenario is hypothetical and labelled on screen: "A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO". All prices are invented and
  labelled "ILLUSTRATIVE PRICES - A HYPOTHETICAL ECONOMY"; the graph is labelled "ILLUSTRATIVE - NOT REAL DATA".
- The economics is stated cautiously, in line with the IMF, the ECB and Federal Reserve education material: when
  money multiplies but the amount of food, houses and cars does not, prices rise and each dollar buys less. The video
  does not claim inflation always works this way; sources.txt explains the caveats (velocity, spare capacity,
  supply shocks).
- "Too much money, chasing too few goods" is used as a standard textbook phrase, not attributed to anyone.
- The banknote is an invented design and copies no real currency. All characters, brands and places are invented.
