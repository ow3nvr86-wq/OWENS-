WHAT IF MONEY GREW ON TREES? - SOURCES, FACT TABLE AND HONESTY NOTES
=======================================================================

This Short is a HYPOTHETICAL comedy. Money does not grow on trees, nothing in the story happened, and every price
in it is invented. The only factual content is the economic idea it ends on, and it is stated in the cautious form
that central banks and the IMF use. The video says so on screen ("A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO", "ILLUSTRATIVE PRICES -
A HYPOTHETICAL ECONOMY", "ILLUSTRATIVE - NOT REAL DATA" on the graph).

[8] Centre for Future Work, "We need more goods, not less money" (2023) - an example of the counter-view that recent

3. CONFIRMED vs DISPUTED vs SPECULATION
---------------------------------------
CONFIRMED (mainstream textbook / central-bank position):
- When the amount of money and spending grows much faster than the economy's ability to produce goods and services,
  prices tend to rise and each unit of money buys less [1] [2] [3] [4].
- Real hyperinflations (Germany 1923) produced the "wheelbarrow of cash" image [6] [7].

DISPUTED / NUANCED (and how the script handles it):
- Inflation does not always follow money growth one-for-one. How fast money is spent (velocity) changes; extra money
  can be saved rather than spent; and if an economy has idle capacity, output can rise too [1] [5].
- Inflation can also come from the supply side (shortages, energy shocks), not only from "too much money". The causes
  of the 2021-2023 inflation are still argued over [8].
- The script therefore keeps to the scenario it describes - a world where money multiplies while the amount of food,
  houses and cars stays the same - and avoids saying that inflation "always" works this way. It never names a
  real-world inflation rate or blames a real government.

SPECULATION / FICTION:
- The whole premise (money growing on trees, farms switching to money orchards, a man buying a forest) is invented
  for comedy. No real people, companies or events are depicted.


4. WHAT IS ILLUSTRATIVE (RECREATIONS, NOT RECORDS)
--------------------------------------------------
- All characters are stylised, invented figures; none represents a real person.
- The banknote is an invented design ("LEGAL TENDER", a tree in the oval, "THIS NOTE GREW ON A TREE",
  serial "TR 0000001 EE"). It deliberately copies no real currency: no real portrait, seal, motto, signature or
  serial format.
- Store, brands and signs are invented ("CORNER GROCER", "SUNNY O'S", "BRAN BITS", "LOW PRICES EVERY DAY!" etc.).
- The city screen's graph has no axis values and is labelled ILLUSTRATIVE - NOT REAL DATA.
- Every price is fictional and labelled ILLUSTRATIVE PRICES - A HYPOTHETICAL ECONOMY.


5. FACTS CHECKED BUT CUT FOR TIME
---------------------------------
- Germany, November 1923: one U.S. dollar = one trillion (10^12) paper marks [6].
- Germany, late 1923: a loaf of bread cost billions of marks (figures quoted for November 1923 are around 80 billion)
  [7]; sources differ on the exact monthly prices, so no number was used.
- The phrase usually attributed to Milton Friedman is "inflation is always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon"; the
  "too much money chasing..." wording appears in Friedman and Schwartz's book in a longer form [5]. Because the
  short slogan has no single verified author, the narration does not attribute it to anyone.
- The idea that a currency's value depends on how much there is of it compared with goods is the "quantity theory of
  money" [1]; the name was cut to keep the narration simple.
