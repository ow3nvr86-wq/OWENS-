CHERNOBYL: THE NIGHT THAT CHANGED HISTORY
A 60-second vertical dark-history Short in 2D illustrated animation, made entirely with code
===========================================================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 59.8 s, H.264 + AAC. At 1:23 a.m. on 26 April 1986 a safety test on Reactor 4 of the
Chernobyl plant in Soviet Ukraine ended in a power surge, two explosions and a fire. Firefighters and plant workers
went in; in Pripyat a few kilometres away, Saturday went on. About 36 hours later the buses came, and the residents
never returned; about 116,000 people were evacuated in 1986. The film ends on the steel arch over the reactor and
the words "APRIL 26, 1986 / CHERNOBYL CHANGED THE WORLD."

2D ONLY: no Blender, no 3D models, no 3D renderer or camera. Same engine and look as Dancing_Plague_1518/ and
Lost_Colony_Roanoke/: every frame is drawn with pycairo as flat vector shapes with ink outlines, cel shadows and
engraving hatches; numpy adds paper, grain, fog and the grade; Pillow draws the captions; FFmpeg encodes.
(The brief suggested Pillow or Pygame; pycairo is used for drawing because it gives anti-aliased vector ink lines
at any zoom. Pillow is used for the captions and titles, FFmpeg for the video.)

THE 2D TECHNIQUES
-----------------
- Characters: a puppet rig per character driven by pose functions (walk cycles at different speeds, running,
  idles with breathing and blinking, talking, pointing, writing, folded arms, reactions, a cyclist pedalling,
  firefighters holding a spraying hose, residents carrying suitcases), on twos with line boil; no two people share
  a design.
- Backgrounds in depth layers moved by a multiplane camera; the planes the camera pushes into are drawn live.
- Animated environment: blinking aviation lights, reflections on the cooling pond, the fireball and debris, rising
  and leaning smoke columns lit from below, flickering fires, drifting embers, sweeping searchlights, flashing
  beacons, moving traffic and buses with turning wheels, swaying trees, a bouncing ball, a dial's needle, scrolling
  chart recorders, flashing lamps, flickering ceiling lights, camera shake, blowing leaves, rocking gondolas and a
  moving swing, drifting fog.
- Transitions: white flash, timber foreground wipes, cross dissolves, a page turn, an ink spread, a fade to black.


HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
  cd Chernobyl_1986
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py              # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, build/vo_timing.json
  $SHORTS_PY src/sets.py                     # background layers (about 80 s)
  $SHORTS_PY -c "import sys; sys.path.insert(0,'src'); import post; post.save_paper()"
  $SHORTS_PY audio.py                        # score, ambience, effects, mixes, sound_design.txt
  $SHORTS_PY render.py --stills 1.5,8,12.5,21 # look-dev stills -> build/stills/
  $SHORTS_PY render.py --preview             # -> renders/preview.mp4 (about 5 minutes)
  $SHORTS_PY render.py                       # -> final_video.mp4 (about 12-15 minutes on 4 cores)
  $SHORTS_PY render.py --no-voice            # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4
  $SHORTS_PY tools/export_assets.py          # character and prop sheets
Requirements: Python 3 with numpy, scipy, pillow, pycairo; ffmpeg; kokoro-onnx for the voice.


ACCURACY AND CARE
-----------------
- Every narrated claim is checked in sources.txt (section 4) against the IAEA, the OECD NEA, the WHO, the World
  Nuclear Association and Ukrainian historical sources.
- Careful wording: "tens of thousands" for Pripyat (sources give 43,000-49,000); "many residents" had little idea
  of the danger (not all); the cause as "a flawed design and operating errors combined" (no blame is placed on any
  person); 116,000 evacuated in 1986 (the IAEA's 1987 figure was 115,000).
- No death toll is stated, because long-term estimates differ widely; no injuries are shown; no real person is
  portrayed, named or quoted; no dialogue or documents are invented.
- Artistic reconstruction (see sources.txt section 5): the plant, control room, city and funfair are illustrations
  in the style of the place and period, not plans; the years of abandonment are compressed.
