2. VERIFIED TIMELINE
--------------------
25-26 Apr 1986  Unit 4 (an RBMK-1000) is brought down for a test of whether, after a loss of station power, the slowing
                turbine could keep the cooling pumps running until the diesels started. [NEA02, WNA1]
26 Apr, 01:23   The power surges; the emergency button is pressed at 01:23:40; two explosions destroy the reactor and
                the building; fires start. [IAEA87, WNA1]
01:28           The first group of 14 firefighters arrives. [WNA1]
26 Apr          Two plant workers die on the night; 134 plant staff and emergency workers later develop acute
                radiation syndrome, and 28 of them die within months. (Not stated in the video.) [CNSC, WHO05]
26 Apr          Saturday: life in Pripyat continues while the emergency goes on at the plant. [IAEA87 implicitly; general]
27 Apr, 14:00   About 36 hours after the accident the evacuation of Pripyat begins; it is complete within about three
                hours. The evacuation was presented as temporary; residents never returned to live there. [IAEA87, search
                summaries of contemporary accounts]
1986            About 116,000 people evacuated (IAEA 1987 gave 115,000), from 188 settlements. [UHJ, ICRP, ChNPP, IAEA87]
May-Nov 1986    The 'Shelter' (sarcophagus) is built over the ruined unit. [NG16]
2013            The ventilation stack beside Unit 4 is dismantled (why the arch scene shows no stack).
29 Nov 2016     The New Safe Confinement, a steel arch 108 m high with a 257 m span, is slid over the reactor. [AHF]


3. CAUSES (as said in the video: "a flawed design and operating errors combined")
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The NEA and IAEA accounts attribute the accident to the combination of the RBMK reactor's design weaknesses and the
way the test was prepared and carried out. The video does not apportion blame or show the AZ-5 button, and shows no
specific person's actions or words.


4. CLAIM-BY-CLAIM CHECK OF THE NARRATION AND TEXT
------------------------------------------------
"At 1:23 in the morning, a nuclear reactor in Soviet Ukraine... was destroyed."  [IAEA87, WNA1]
    On screen: "Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant, Soviet Ukraine", "26 April 1986, 01:23".
"A few kilometres away, tens of thousands of people lived ordinary lives in the city of Pripyat."
    Pripyat's population is given as about 43,000-49,000 in different sources, so "tens of thousands".
"That night, during a safety test on Reactor 4, a flawed design and operating errors combined... and the power surged."
    [NEA02, WNA1].
"Two explosions tore the reactor open. The fire released radioactive material into the air."  [WNA1, IAEA87]
"Firefighters and plant workers rushed in, facing an emergency whose full scale was not yet understood."
    [WNA1] (first crew at 01:28). On screen: "The first fire crews arrived within minutes".
"That Saturday, life in Pripyat carried on. Many residents had little idea of the danger nearby."
    26 April 1986 was a Saturday. "Many", not "all": knowledge differed between people.
"About thirty-six hours after the explosion, the buses came. Residents were told the evacuation was temporary."
    [IAEA87] and contemporary accounts.
"They never came back. In 1986 alone, about 116,000 people were evacuated."
    Pripyat was never resettled. 116,000 [UHJ, ICRP, ChNPP]; IAEA (1987) said 115,000. On screen: "116,000 people
    evacuated in 1986, from 188 settlements".
"Its consequences are still felt today, and it changed how the world sees nuclear safety."
    General conclusion of the IAEA, NEA and WHO reports; no number of victims is given in the video, because
    estimates of long-term deaths differ widely and are debated [WHO05].


5. WHAT IS ARTISTIC RECONSTRUCTION
----------------------------------
- Every person is invented; no real person is portrayed, named or quoted, and no words are put in anyone's mouth.
- The plant is a simplified elevation (turbine hall, reactor buildings, the stack); it is not to scale. The damaged
  building, the fires and the smoke are an illustration, not a reconstruction of the exact damage.
- The control room is a generic illustration of an RBMK unit control room (panels, dials, chart recorders, a large
  lamp display); the dial's label (MW) is illustrative.
- The explosion is shown as a burst of light and debris from a distance; no injuries are shown.
- Pripyat's streets, school, buses and the funfair are drawn in the style of the place and period, not from plans.
- The years of abandonment (trees, grass, broken windows) are compressed into a few seconds; the overhead view is
  an illustration.


6. FACTS CUT FOR TIME
---------------------
The AZ-5 button; the exact test sequence; the graphite fire's duration (reported as about nine or ten days, while
INSAG-1 says the fires were put out by 05:00); the 30-km exclusion zone; the detection of fallout in Sweden on
28 April; the 28 acute radiation deaths; the liquidators; the new city of Slavutych.
