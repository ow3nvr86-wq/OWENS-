WHAT IF YOU WERE THE LAST HUMAN ON EARTH? - OPENING HOOK OPTIONS
=================================================================

Scored 1-5 on: stops the scroll in 1-2 s, works with the sound off, puts the viewer INSIDE the experience, originality,
open loop.

OPTION 1 (first-person anti-hook: the phone) - SELECTED
  Picture: an extreme close-up of a pair of eyes opening, lit only by a phone screen. Cut: the phone - 07:12, "No
  notifications". Cut: the same face at a 12th-floor window; the camera pulls back over the shoulder and the city
  below is completely empty - cars stopped in the lanes, nobody on the pavements, nothing moving.
  On screen: NO CALLS. NO MESSAGES.  ->  YOU ARE THE LAST HUMAN ON EARTH.  /  EVERYONE IS GONE.
  Narration: "You wake up. No calls. No messages. The city outside is silent. Everyone is gone."
  Stop-the-scroll 5 | Sound-off 5 | Inside the experience 5 | Originality 4 | Open loop 5   = 24/25
  - Everyone has woken up and checked their phone; "no messages at all" is a small, instantly recognisable wrongness,
    and it is the viewer's own morning routine (second person: "you").
  - The window reveal pays it off within 5 seconds with one huge image, so nobody is left confused.
  - The phone becomes the thread of the whole film: the last scene is the same phone, "No service", and the last shot
    is the same eyes closing - so the Short loops back to the eyes opening without forcing it.

OPTION 2 (event anti-hook: the crowded street)
  Picture: a busy pedestrian crossing at rush hour - then a hard cut on a blink to the same crossing, completely empty;
  the crossing signal still ticks.
  Narration: "Blink. And eight billion people are gone."
  Stop-the-scroll 4 | Sound-off 4 | Inside the experience 3 | Originality 3 | Open loop 4   = 18/25
  - A strong before/after, but it needs a crowd in the first second (expensive, and it shows the event from outside
    rather than through the character's eyes). The ticking crossing signal survives as the "something is wrong" beat.

OPTION 3 (traditional hook: the question)
  Picture: an aerial shot of a silent city at dawn.
  Narration: "If every other person on Earth vanished tonight, how long would it take you to notice the world falling
  apart?"
  Stop-the-scroll 3 | Sound-off 2 | Inside the experience 2 | Originality 3 | Open loop 5   = 15/25
  - The best open loop, but it explains before it shows, and a pretty aerial shot is easy to scroll past. Its question
    became the structure of the film instead (exciting first half -> the systems start to fail -> the silence).

SELECTED: OPTION 1 - first person, instant, readable with the sound off, and it sets up the loop.

OPEN LOOP: "Everyone is gone" (5 s) raises two questions the film answers in order: is it as good as it first seems
(scene 2: "The entire world is yours."), and what happens next ("But something is wrong." at 17 s -> the lights going
out at 29 s -> the silence at 33 s -> the realisation on the roof at 43 s -> "nobody left to tell" at 55 s).

Phrases avoided on purpose: "Have you ever wondered...", "Today we're going to explore...", "You won't believe what
happens...", "Imagine if I told you...".

THE TWO PROPOSED ENDINGS
  A: "You wouldn't own the world. You'd just be the only one left to experience it."
  B: "The strangest part wouldn't be having the entire planet to yourself. It would be realising there's nobody left
      to tell."  - CHOSEN (lightly shortened: "...having the world to yourself...")
  B wins because it lands on the film's thread - the phone with no messages - and turns the opening's "No calls. No
  messages." from a puzzle into the emotional point. It is followed by one short question to the viewer: "Would you
  still want it?"
