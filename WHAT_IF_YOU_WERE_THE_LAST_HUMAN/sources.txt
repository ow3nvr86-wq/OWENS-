WHAT IF YOU WERE THE LAST HUMAN ON EARTH? - SOURCES, FACT TABLE AND HONESTY NOTES
==================================================================================

This Short is FICTION: a hypothetical "what if" told in the second person. Nothing in it happened, and it does not
explain how everyone disappears. It is labelled on screen ("A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO"). What it tries to get right is
how the world around the last person would behave in the first day: things that run automatically keep running for
a while, things that need people to watch over them start to fail - gradually, not all at once - and nature carries on.

The narration makes only a few factual claims. Each was checked in at least two sources.


1. SOURCES
----------
[1] United Nations, Department of Economic and Social Affairs, "World Population Prospects 2024" press release
    (11 July 2024): world population 8.2 billion in 2024, projected to peak at about 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s.
    https://www.un.org/sustainabledevelopment/blog/2024/07/press-release-wpp2024/
[2] UN News, "World population expected to reach 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s" (July 2024); UN Geneva, "Growing or
    shrinking? What latest trends tell us about world's population" (July 2024) - same 8.2 billion figure.
    https://news.un.org/en/story/2024/07/1151971
    https://www.ungeneva.org/en/news-media/news/2024/07/95264/growing-or-shrinking-what-latest-trends-tell-us-about-worlds
[3] U.S. Department of Energy, "The Role of the Balancing Authority" (and its 2022 Balancing Authority backgrounder):
    a balancing authority "ensures, in real time, that power system demand and supply are finely balanced"; if they
    drift apart, frequency deviations can damage equipment and cause wide blackouts; operators can shed load (rolling
    blackouts) to restore the balance.
    https://www.energy.gov/documents/role-balancing-authority
[4] U.S. Energy Information Administration, "Today in Energy": "U.S. electric system is made up of interconnections and
    balancing authorities" - the actual operation of the electric system is managed by balancing authorities.
    https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=27152
[5] North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC), Reliability Standard PER-004: each Reliability Coordinator
    "shall be staffed with adequately trained and NERC-certified Reliability Coordinator operators, 24 hours per day,
    seven days per week"; PER-003: real-time system operators must be NERC-certified.
    https://nerc.com/pa/Stand/Reliability%20Standards/PER-004-0.pdf
    https://www.nerc.com/pa/Stand/Reliability%20Standards/PER-003-2.pdf
[6] U.S. Federal Highway Administration, Traffic Signal Timing Manual, chapter 5: signals run on "pre-timed" control
    (fixed intervals, regardless of traffic) or "actuated" control (phases called by vehicle detectors).
    https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop08024/chapter5.htm
[7] Backup power at mobile phone sites: after Hurricane Katrina the FCC set an 8-hour backup-power expectation for
    cell sites (contested in court by carriers); runtimes in practice vary from a few hours (batteries) to days
    (generators). NBC News, "Some cell carriers fight FCC backup power rule" (2008); KTVU, "California could mandate
    backup power at cell phone towers" (2019).
    https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna23523577
    https://www.ktvu.com/news/california-could-mandate-backup-power-at-cell-phone-towers
[8] USDA food-safety guidance on power cuts (via UConn Extension and USDA): a closed refrigerator keeps food cold for
    about 4 hours; a full freezer about 48 hours (24 if half full).
    https://publications.extension.uconn.edu/2014/02/19/keeping-food-safe-during-a-power-outage
    https://www.usda.gov/node/44742

3. CONFIRMED vs ESTIMATED vs SPECULATION
----------------------------------------
CONFIRMED (facts):
- World population: about 8.2 billion (2024) [1] [2].
- Power grids are balanced continuously by staffed control rooms; NERC requires certified operators around the clock [3] [4] [5].
- Many traffic signals run on fixed timers [6]; phone sites have limited backup power [7].
REASONABLE EXPECTATION (presented as story, without times or numbers):
- That grids would fail somewhere "sooner or later" without operators, area by area rather than everywhere at once.
- That some lights, screens, escalators, fountains and fridges keep running while their area still has power.
SPECULATION / NOT CLAIMED:
- How long any particular system would last. Real answers depend on the plant, the fuel, the weather and the
  automation; the film deliberately gives no timeline ("for a while", "sooner or later").
- What caused everyone to disappear - the story never says, and it is not a scientific claim.
- Animals: pigeons appear in the square because animals would still be there; nothing is claimed about them.


4. WHAT IS ILLUSTRATIVE (made up for the story)
-----------------------------------------------
- The city, its streets, shops ("LUNA CAFE", "HARBOUR BAKERY", "FRESH MARKET", "PAGE & SPINE BOOKS"), the supermarket,
  mall, cinema, station and every sign, screen and departure board are invented.
- The main character is an invented, stylised figure; nobody real is depicted. The "old photograph" shows invented,
  stylised figures, not real people.
- The order and speed of the lights going out at dusk are compressed for the story.
- The film shown in the empty cinema is an abstract landscape made for this video.


5. FACTS CHECKED BUT CUT FOR TIME
---------------------------------
- Food: a closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours without power, a full freezer about 48 hours [8] - so fresh
  food in shops would start to spoil soon after their power failed, while tins and dried food would last for years.
- Phone networks: the FCC's post-Katrina 8-hour backup expectation for cell sites [7] - cut because the rule's current
  status could not be confirmed and real runtimes vary widely.
- Detector-actuated traffic signals can "rest" on one colour when no vehicle is detected [6] - true, but too detailed.
- UN projection: world population is expected to peak at about 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s [1].
