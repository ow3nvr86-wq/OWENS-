WHAT IF YOU WERE THE LAST HUMAN ON EARTH?
A 59-second vertical Short, made entirely with code
=================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 59.0 s, H.264 + AAC. Told in the second person ("you"), following ONE ordinary young
adult - the only person in the film. They wake up: no calls, no messages. From their 12th-floor window the city is
empty. At first it is wonderful - the avenue, a supermarket, a cinema, a mall, a square full of pigeons are all
theirs. Then things that need people start to go wrong: traffic lights change for no one, a train waits with its doors
open, a neighbour's dinner sits under the lights, and at dusk whole districts go dark one after another. Night brings
the silence. At dawn, on the roof of their tower, the whole city is theirs - and there is nobody to share it with.
The phone, one last time: no service, no new messages. Their eyes close, mirroring the first shot, so the Short loops.

Everything you see and hear is generated by code:
- Visuals: Blender's Python API (bpy) builds and renders every frame: the main character (one consistent model in
  every shot: short dark hair, olive hoodie, jeans, white trainers, an animated face), their flat (bedroom + kitchen)
  and the roof of their tower, a city avenue with shops, a cafe, abandoned cars, a bus, working traffic signals, a
  plaza with a running fountain and pigeons, a park with a playground, a procedural city grid out to a downtown
  skyline (windows and street lamps belong to "power districts" that can go dark), a supermarket, a shopping mall with
  a moving escalator, a cinema with the film still playing, a railway station with a train waiting and a neighbour's
  lit, empty home. No stock footage, downloaded models, textures or templates.
- Printed props: tools/textures.py (Pillow) draws the shop/mall/cinema/station signs, both phone screens, the old
  photograph (invented, stylised figures), the departure board, the film and TV pictures and the shelf products.
- Captions: tools/captions.py (Pillow): word-by-word captions plus five large "key" captions.
- Music and sound: numpy synthesis in animation.py --stage audio. No samples or recordings.
- Narration: a synthetic voice (Kokoro-82M, voice "bm_george"), made by tools/voiceover.py.


FILES
-----
  script.txt          the narration with measured start/end times (136 words)
  hooks.txt           three opening hooks (anti-hooks and a traditional one), scores, the chosen hook, the open loop,
                      and the choice between the two proposed endings
  storyboard.txt      scene-by-scene storyboard: visuals, character action, camera, lighting, sound, narration,
                      on-screen text, key captions, transitions (generated from scene_timing.json by tools/voiceover.py)
  animation.py        the whole production in one Blender script: sets, character, animation, cameras, lighting,
                      render, audio, edit (titles, key captions, captions, dips, fade) and encode. Objects are organised
                      in named collections (City, Apt, Chars, Market, Mall, Cinema, Station, House, NightLights, FX, Rig,
                      Lights) and built by reusable functions (building, car_template, signal_head, room_box,
                      city_facade_lit, camera_shot, build_char, pose, walk_along, reach, look_at, ...)
  subtitles.srt       subtitles timed from the synthesised voice (short phrases, max 3 words)
  sound_design.txt    the sound idea, every music and sound cue with its time, and a music guide for re-scoring
  sources.txt         sources, claim table, confirmed vs expected vs speculation, what is invented
  README.txt          this file
  final_video.mp4     the finished Short (narration, captions, music and sound)

  scene_timing.json   the single source of truth for timing (scenes, shots, narration, titles, key captions)
  render_all.sh       resumable full render in 200-frame chunks
  tools/              voiceover.py, captions.py, textures.py, contact.py (look-dev contact sheets)
  audio/              narration.wav, mix_with_voice.wav (final mix), mix_no_voice.wav (music + sound only)
  renders/            preview.mp4 (low-resolution preview), final_video_no_voice.mp4 (no narration or captions,
                      for recording your own voice)
  assets/fonts/       Anton, Old Standard TT, Covered By Your Grace (SIL OFL)


THE SIX SCENES (times follow the measured voice; 23 shots, every one with camera movement)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  0.0-7.5   YOU WAKE UP            Extreme close-up of the eyes opening by phone light; the phone (07:12, "No
                                   notifications"); at the window the camera pulls back over the shoulder to reveal the
                                   empty avenue. YOU ARE THE LAST HUMAN ON EARTH. / NO CALLS. NO MESSAGES. / EVERYONE IS GONE.
  7.5-16.9  THE WORLD IS YOURS     Walking down the centre line between abandoned cars; tossing an apple in an empty
                                   supermarket; alone in the cinema with popcorn; riding the mall escalator; spinning in
                                   the square as pigeons take off. THE ENTIRE WORLD IS YOURS.
  16.9-32.1 SOMETHING IS WRONG     A traffic light changes for no one (they still wait for green); a train waits with its
                                   doors open; a neighbour's lit home with dinner on the table; sunset over the skyline;
                                   at blue hour, districts go dark one by one. BUT SOMETHING IS WRONG.
  32.1-42.4 THE SILENCE            Night: the old photograph at the kitchen table; paper blowing across the empty avenue
                                   (two seconds with no narration and no music); alone on a swing beside an empty one;
                                   a tiny figure walking between dark towers. YOU ARE COMPLETELY ALONE.
  42.4-49.4 THE BIG REALISATION    Dawn: the roof door opens; a huge aerial pull-back reveals the whole empty city.
  49.4-59.0 THE FINAL PAYOFF       The phone: "No service", "No new messages". Into the pocket; a long look at the
                                   horizon; extreme close-up of the eyes closing (fade to black -> loops to the eyes
                                   opening).


HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3), ffmpeg, Python 3 with Pillow and numpy (for tools/textures.py and
tools/captions.py). The voice step needs Python with kokoro-onnx and soundfile, plus kokoro-v1.0.onnx and
voices-v1.0.bin from the kokoro-onnx releases. In this repository's Claude Code cloud sessions, the SessionStart hook
installs all of it and exports $BLENDER, $SHORTS_PY, $KOKORO_PY and $KOKORO_DIR.

  cd WHAT_IF_YOU_WERE_THE_LAST_HUMAN
  $SHORTS_PY tools/textures.py                      # printed props -> build/tex/ (animation.py also runs it if missing)
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, storyboard.txt
  $SHORTS_PY tools/captions.py                      # caption images (the edit stage also runs it if missing)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 28,179,486,924,1362,1734
                                                    # look-dev stills (frame numbers; --out to choose a folder)
  $SHORTS_PY tools/contact.py renders/frames sheet.jpg 6      # contact sheet of the stills
  ./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
  ./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/ (resumable)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                  # -> final_video.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice       # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --out renders/preview_frames --output preview.mp4 --bitrate 3500

To inspect the generated 3D scene in Blender's interface: blender -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality
preview --stills 1 --out build/inspect --save-blend build/scene.blend, then open build/scene.blend. (The saved file holds
every set, the character and all camera rigs; the per-frame animation is applied by the script's frame handler while
rendering, so the saved file shows the scene as it is at frame 1.)

On Windows or macOS, replace $BLENDER with the path to blender.exe or Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender and run
render_all.sh's loop by hand (or simply: blender -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality final).

To edit in CapCut instead: render the frames, run the audio stage, then import renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4 (or the
frames plus audio/mix_no_voice.wav), add audio/narration.wav, and use subtitles.srt for the captions. The five key
captions are listed in scene_timing.json ("keys") and in storyboard.txt.

To retime: edit scene_timing.json, re-run tools/voiceover.py and tools/captions.py, then render, audio and edit.
Re-render only the shots whose 3D content or timing you changed.

RENDER TIMES (4 CPU cores, no GPU, Cycles CPU)
----------------------------------------------
  Look-dev stills (270x480, 4 samples)       about 2-6 s per frame, plus about 1 min of scene building per run
  Preview, 1,770 frames (270x480, 4 samples)  about 2 s per frame, about 45 min in total (plus a short re-render of two
                                              ranges after a silent crash; render_all.sh resumes, so only missing
                                              frames are rendered again)
  Final, 1,770 frames (540x960, 6 samples)    6.3 s per frame on average (4.0 s fastest, 10 s slowest; 80 % of
                                              frames between 5.2 and 7.7 s), 3 h 10 min in total; it ran as two background runs
                                              because one command may run for at most 2 hours, resuming at frame 1050
  Audio stage                                 about 1 min
  Edit stage (final_video.mp4)                3 min 35 s
  Edit stage --no-voice                       3 min 11 s

  final_video.mp4                   1080x1920, 30 fps, 59.0 s, H.264 + AAC 48 kHz, 58.9 MB, -14.0 LUFS (peak -2.2 dBFS)
  renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4  1080x1920, 30 fps, 59.0 s, H.264 + AAC 48 kHz, 60.0 MB, -14.0 LUFS (peak -1.7 dBFS)


QUALITY CHECK (against the brief)
---------------------------------
- First second: an extreme close-up of eyes opening in the dark by phone light, then "NO CALLS. NO MESSAGES." over the
  phone; the empty city is revealed by 5 s and named on screen (YOU ARE THE LAST HUMAN ON EARTH., EVERYONE IS GONE.).
- One consistent character: the same model (built once by build_char) in all 20 shots that show a person.
- Progression: freedom (7-17 s, light music, smiles) -> unease (17-32 s, the music stops, systems failing gradually)
  -> silence (32-42 s) -> realisation (42-49 s) -> payoff (49-59 s). Expressions go puzzled -> grinning -> uneasy -> sad -> calm.
- Every shot has camera movement and something moving in the world (walking, the escalator, pigeons, the fountain, a
  changing signal, flickering screens, paper in the wind, swings, the outage, the door, the aerial pull-back).
- Captions: Anton, white with a black outline, active word amber, max 3 words, about 64% down the frame; key captions
  larger and amber. Titles at the top on a dark plate.
- Sound: no human voices except the narrator; strategic silence at about 35.4-37.4 s; -14 LUFS.


ACCURACY AND HONESTY
--------------------
- The story is fiction and labelled on screen ("A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO"). It never explains how everyone vanished.
- Facts in the narration are checked (sources.txt): over 8 billion people (UN, 8.2 billion in 2024); power grids are
  balanced in real time by control rooms staffed around the clock.
- The film shows a gradual, patchy failure - signals still cycling, a train's lights on, some districts going dark
  while others stay lit - and gives no timeline for any system ("for a while", "sooner or later"). Buildings, roads and
  nature are unchanged: it is the first day, not centuries of decay. Animals are still there (pigeons, a distant dog).
- The city, shops, brands, the photograph and the character are invented; people are stylised figures; nobody real is
  depicted.
