# YouTube Short: "What If You Fell Into Jupiter?"

A Channel Short from the `ai-video-director` agent. It is the first episode of a
"What if you fell into..." series (see section 10).

## 1. Brief

| | |
|---|---|
| Platform | YouTube Shorts. 9:16, 1080x1920 |
| Length | About 42 seconds |
| Goal | Views, rewatches and subscribers |
| Style | Photoreal space cinematic, as if shot by a camera falling with the astronaut |
| Topic | Falling into Jupiter, from orbit down to the deep interior |

**Why this topic:** nobody can film it for real, so AI footage is the only way to show it.
The format has a built-in reason to keep watching ("how deep do you get?"), and every beat is
a real fact.

## 2. Concept

**Hook (0–2s):** *"You just fell into Jupiter."* plus the on-screen text **HOW LONG WOULD YOU
LAST?**

- **Hook:** an astronaut tumbles toward a Jupiter that fills the whole frame.
- **Proof:** the fall happens in layers: the speed, the entry, the Great Red Spot, ammonia
  clouds, water clouds with lightning, the real 1995 probe, liquid gas, metallic hydrogen.
  A depth counter in the corner keeps climbing.
- **Payoff and loop:** *"...and you'd never hit the ground, because there's no ground to
  hit."* This cuts straight back to the opening shot, so the Short loops.

## 3. Hero frame (character reference)

Generate this still first with CapCut's AI image tool, then attach it as the reference to every
astronaut shot. The mirrored visor hides the face, which keeps the character consistent from
shot to shot.

> Full-body astronaut in a bulky white spacesuit with a gold mirrored visor and a small orange
> stripe on each arm, floating in black space, Jupiter's cream and rust-brown cloud bands
> reflected in the visor, hard sunlight from the left, photoreal, IMAX cinematic, 9:16 vertical

**Subject line to reuse word for word in every astronaut shot:**
`an astronaut in a bulky white spacesuit with a gold mirrored visor and a small orange stripe on each arm`

**Negative prompt for every shot:** `text, watermark, logo, flag, name tag, visible face, extra limbs, distorted hands, cartoon, low detail`

## 7. Music and sound

- From CapCut's built-in library (licensed for YouTube), search "space ambient" or
  "cinematic tension" for a low drone that builds.
- **0–2.5s:** wind roar and a breathing sound effect inside the helmet, with almost no music.
- **5.5s:** a big impact sound effect plus a crackling-fire sound on entry.
- **16s:** thunder hits, timed to each lightning flash.
- **24s:** cut the music completely, add radio static and a flatline beep for "signal lost".
- **27s on:** the music comes back deeper and heavier, rising to the whiteout.
- **39s:** dead silence on the black frame, then the shot 1 wind roar comes back for the loop.
- Duck the music to about 25% under the voiceover.

## 8. CapCut build steps

1. In CapCut desktop, click **New project** and set the ratio to **9:16**.
2. Go to **AI media → AI image** and generate the hero frame from section 3. Save it.
3. Go to **AI media → AI video**. For shots 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 9, use **Image to video** with
   the hero frame attached. For shots 2, 7, 8, 10 and 11, use **Text to video**. Paste in the
   prompt and the negative prompt. Generate two takes of each shot and keep the best one.
4. Drag the clips onto the timeline in order and trim them to the shot list times.
5. Shot 12: duplicate the first 1 second of shot 1 and put it at the end, after a 0.3s black
   frame.
6. Add a **speed ramp** to shot 2 (**Speed → Curve → Bullet** or **Montage**) so it accelerates.
7. Add a **camera shake** effect to shot 3, and a **glitch** effect to the end of shot 8.
8. Add the voiceover lines with **Text → Text to speech**, then line each one up with its
   time.
9. Run **Captions → Auto captions** and restyle them as described in section 6.
10. Add the hook text and the depth counter as **Text** layers. For the counter, make one text
    layer per value and cut between them in time with the shots.
11. Add the music and sound effects from section 7, and duck the music under the voice.
12. Watch it through twice. On the second pass, check that the end loops cleanly back to the
    start.
13. Click **Export** at 1080p, 30fps, high bitrate. Save as `fall-into-jupiter.mp4`.

## 10. Sources and series

**Facts used:**

- Escape velocity of 59.5 km/s, about 214,000 km/h: [NASA Jupiter Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/jupiterfact.html)
- The Galileo probe entered on 7 December 1995, transmitted for 57.6 minutes and fell about
  150 km, ending at about 22 bar and 153 °C: [Galileo Probe "Quick-look" Science Summary](https://astro.sci.muni.cz/pub/galileo/first_science_summary.html)
- The Great Red Spot is shrinking but still wider than Earth: [NASA, "A Storm of Change"](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/a-storm-of-change/)
- No solid surface, ammonia and water cloud layers, and metallic hydrogen deeper down:
  [NASA Jupiter facts](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-facts/)
- The core is hotter than the Sun's surface (about 5,500 °C). Estimates for the core run from
  roughly 16,000 K to over 30,000 °C, and every estimate clears that bar: [Universe Today,
  "Temperature of Jupiter"](https://www.universetoday.com/15097/temperature-of-jupiter/)
