How a Rocket Cart Stopped an Army - fact check (every on-screen claim checked in 2+ sources)

Claim (as narrated)                         | Used                 | Sources / notes
1593, Korea, hilltop fort called Haengju     | early 1593           | Wikipedia "Battle of Haengju"; Korea Times. Date given as 14 March 1593 (Gregorian) or 12 Feb (lunar) - narration says only "1593".
Japanese army of "around thirty thousand"    | ~30,000              | Wikipedia "Battle of Haengju"; Warfare History Network. Most-cited figure; estimates vary, hence "around".
"About three thousand defenders"             | ~2,300-3,000         | Wikipedia "Battle of Haengju"; Korea Times. Hence "about"; the 10:1 odds are therefore "roughly".
General Kwon Yul, refused to leave           | Kwon Yul             | Wikipedia "Battle of Haengju"; Korea Times.
Hwacha: cart with a honeycomb launcher       | yes                  | Wikipedia "Hwacha"; The National Interest. The 1451 (Munjong) model fired singijeon rocket arrows.
"Up to a hundred arrows" at once             | up to 100            | Wikipedia "Hwacha"; The National Interest. Some sources say up to 200 with other launcher variants; "up to a hundred" is the conservative, commonly cited number.
Repeated charges, Japanese army withdrew     | yes                  | Wikipedia "Battle of Haengju"; Warfare History Network.
Kwon later credited the hwachas              | yes                  | Wikipedia "Hwacha" / "Battle of Haengju"; Korea Times. Paraphrased ("gave the carts the credit"), no quote invented.

Illustrative, not historical: the layout of the fort and slope, the character designs, costumes and hats (original
designs, not real people), the number of figures on screen, and the "sneezes rockets" joke line.
Defenders also used other weapons (rocks, arrows, stones thrown by women in the "haengjuchima" legend - omitted as disputed).
