How do you stop thirty thousand soldiers... with a cart?
It's 1593, in Korea. A Japanese army of around thirty thousand marches on a small hilltop fort called Haengju.
Inside are about three thousand defenders, a wooden wall, and a general named Kwon Yul who refuses to leave.
The odds? Roughly ten to one. Not great.
But Kwon Yul has a secret weapon. It looks like a farm cart... with a giant honeycomb of holes on top.
It's called a hwacha. Every hole holds a rocket-powered arrow.
Light one fuse, and up to a hundred arrows scream out at once.
The attackers charge up the hill, wave after wave.
Whoosh. The sky fills with fire, and the charge falls apart.
They charge again, and again. By the end of the day, the huge army pulls back.
Kwon Yul later gave the carts the credit.
Ten to one odds... beaten by a farm cart that sneezes rockets.
