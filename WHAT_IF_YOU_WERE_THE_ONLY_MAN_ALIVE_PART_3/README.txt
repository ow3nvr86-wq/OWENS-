WHAT IF YOU WERE THE ONLY MAN ALIVE? - PART 3
A 30-second vertical Short, made entirely with code
===================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 30.0 s, H.264 + AAC. Part 3 of the series, told in the second person ("you"). The scientists
finally find why you are the only man left - and it isn't on Earth. On a 40-metre holographic timeline, every other
man vanished at the exact same instant, and at that instant a signal arrived from deep space: three short pulses and
one long, repeating. Then the signal changes direction. One continuous dive follows it back to Earth, through the
atmosphere, over the city at night, through the facility roof - down to you, standing in rings of blue light. The
scientists turn. The lead scientist backs away. "Because the signal isn't coming from space anymore... it's coming
from you." Blackout. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT?

Everything you see and hear is generated by code:
- People: the procedural human builder from Parts 1 and 2 (build_human in animation.py). The main character is the same
  figure, built from the same specification (face, skin, eyes, short dark strand hair, stubble, grey T-shirt, dark
  sweatpants, white trainers, height and proportions), with the same ID portrait. The lead scientist, the second
  scientist and the facility director are the same women as in Part 2, from the same specifications. The lab staff are
  baked from the same builder and instanced by geometry nodes.
- The lab is Part 2's research facility (same hall, columns, light lines, consoles, glass room); its wire globe is now a
  holographic Earth, and a 40 m holographic timeline and a 26 m display wall have been added.
- Earth from orbit: a 4096 x 2048 texture drawn by tools/textures.py from coastlines typed in by hand as longitude/latitude
  polygons, roughened with noise and coloured by latitude (forests, deserts, tundra, ice), plus a cloud layer and an
  atmosphere glow; the Moon; a star field with the Milky Way. No map data or photographs are downloaded.
- The signal: one recognisable signature everywhere - three short pulses and one long. It runs along the beams (a shader
  reading a 1-D strip of the pattern), pulses on the screens, rings across the floor around him, and is heard as the
  signal's own tone.
- Sets, lights and cameras: Blender's Python API (bpy). No stock footage, downloaded models, textures or templates.
- Screens: tools/textures.py (Pillow). Captions: tools/captions.py. Music and sound: numpy synthesis in animation.py
  --stage audio, no samples. Narration: Kokoro-82M, voice "bm_george" (the series narrator), made by tools/voiceover.py.

HOW animation.py IS ORGANISED (bpy)
-----------------------------------
- Named collections: Faci (the lab: hall, holographic Earth, timeline, display wall, workstation and vertical monitor,
  the signal's floor rings and light column, the scientists, the instanced staff), Chars (the main character), Space
  (Earth, clouds, atmosphere, Moon, stars, the signal beam and flare), FaciExt (the facility and its city from the air,
  the beam leaving the roof), Booth (portrait booth), FX, Rig (one camera per shot), Lights (sun), PeopleT (baked staff).
  Each shot renders only the collections it needs (SHOTCFG), which keeps every frame cheap.
- Reusable functions: build_human, bake_human, people / instancer (crowds), build_facility, build_lab3, build_space,
  build_faci_exterior, beam (a signal beam of any length), ring_mesh, sphere_obj, geo (longitude/latitude to 3D),
  sph_uv (equirectangular mapping in a shader), glow_add (additive holographic glow), screen_mat, camera_shot (keyed
  path + moving target, bound by a timeline marker), pose, reach, look_at, blend_face, landmark, pulse_33 (the signal's
  rhythm), flicker, beam_phase, update_world.
- Animation is data-driven: a frame_change_pre handler (on_frame) applies the shot's look, poses the people (chor3),
  and drives the screens, the hologram, the timeline flash, the beams, the flare, the floor rings, the light column and
  the failing lights (update_world). The signal's direction is one function: beam_phase (inward until 17.55 s, stopped
  until 17.8 s, then outward).
- The dive "from space to him" is four shots on the same axis (space -> Earth -> the city from 700 m -> the hall from the
  ceiling), each pushing in on the beam, stitched with white-blue flashes in the edit.

HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3), ffmpeg, Python 3 with Pillow and numpy. The voice step needs Python
with kokoro-onnx and soundfile, plus kokoro-v1.0.onnx and voices-v1.0.bin. In this repository's Claude Code cloud
sessions the SessionStart hook installs everything and exports $BLENDER, $SHORTS_PY, $KOKORO_PY and $KOKORO_DIR.

  cd WHAT_IF_YOU_WERE_THE_ONLY_MAN_ALIVE_PART_3
  $SHORTS_PY tools/textures.py                      # screen graphics, Earth, clouds, stars -> build/tex/
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, storyboard.txt
  $SHORTS_PY tools/captions.py                      # caption images (the edit stage also runs it if missing)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 1,60,160,335,475,625,660,745,780
  ./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
  ./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/ (resumable)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                  # -> final_video.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice       # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4

Only frames 1-858 are rendered: after the blackout at 28.15 s the picture is black (scene_timing.json render_end 28.6).
His portrait (build/tex/portrait.png) is the same image as in Parts 1 and 2; if it is missing it is rendered from the 3D
character before the first frame.

To edit in CapCut instead: render the frames, run the audio stage, import renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4 (or the
frames plus audio/mix_no_voice.wav), add audio/narration.wav and use subtitles.srt for the captions.

QUALITY CHECK (the brief's questions)
-------------------------------------
- Does the opening grab attention immediately? Frame 1 is the payoff image (his photo with the pattern glitching on),
  with PART 3 and THEY FOUND SOMETHING. on screen and an impact; the first words start at 0.12 s and say something new.
- Is the mystery understandable? One line per step: they found why -> the same instant -> a signal from space -> it
  changes direction -> it's coming from you. The signal looks and sounds the same everywhere, so it is easy to follow.
- Does the signal reveal surprise? The hook points the viewer to space ("it isn't on Earth"), so the reversal and the
  dive back to him overturn that expectation.
- Is the character consistent? Same build_human specification and the same portrait as Parts 1 and 2; same scientists
  and facility as Part 2.
- Does the story build? Monitor -> lab-wide timeline -> Earth -> deep space -> the dive back -> him.
- Is the narration concise? 75 words, five lines, short sentences.
- Does the ending create curiosity? Nothing is explained - not the signal, the symbol, or why it comes from him.
- Are the visuals animated? Every shot has camera movement; the screens, beams, hologram, rings and lights all animate.
- Is it clearly fictional? Labelled A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO; see sources.txt.
- Exactly 30 seconds? Yes (900 frames at 30 fps).


LIMITATIONS (what this code can and cannot do)
---------------------------------------------
- The people are procedural - no scanned faces, sculpting by hand or facial rig. Faces are smooth and simplified,
  clothes are shells rather than simulated cloth, joints are rotated parts (shoulders and elbows can look segmented
  close up), and the lab coats read a little like tubes at the hips. Gestures use simple inverse kinematics.
- Earth is drawn from rough hand-typed coastlines; it reads as Earth from orbit but is not an accurate map, and its
  texture softens when the dive gets close (the cut to the city happens before that shows). Scale is not realistic.
- "Volumetric" light is faked with additive glowing geometry (beams, columns, rings) rather than true volumes, which
  would multiply the CPU render time; there is no motion blur for the same reason.
- The lab staff are baked, static people. Sound was checked by loudness measurement and timing only.


ACCURACY AND HONESTY
--------------------
- Fiction, labelled on screen. The signal, the symbol, the facility, the people and every number on the screens are
  invented; the cause of the disappearance is never stated. See sources.txt.
