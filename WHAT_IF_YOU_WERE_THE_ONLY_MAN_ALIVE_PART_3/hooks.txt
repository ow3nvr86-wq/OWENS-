WHAT IF YOU WERE THE ONLY MAN ALIVE? PART 3 - OPENING HOOKS AND ENDING
=====================================================================

Three hooks, one per direction in the brief, judged by this production's hook rules: frame 1 must be the payoff image,
the first words must be new and concrete with the twist word last, it must work with the sound off, and the sound must
hit on frame 1. Scores are this production's own (25 = best).

HOOK 1  (the brief's direction 1, sharpened)                                                       (SELECTED)
        Picture, frame 1: a monitor fills the frame - his photo, SUBJECT 001 - and beside it a glowing blue pattern (the
        signal's mark: three short arcs and one long) pulses into existence. PART 3 and THEY FOUND SOMETHING. are on
        screen from frame 1; an impact lands on frame 1. Cut at 1.4 s to an extreme close-up of the lead scientist's
        eyes; at 2.6 s she turns to look at him.
        Words (from 0.12 s): "Scientists just found why you're the only man left... and it isn't on Earth."
        Why it wins: it promises the answer to the series' big question in the first second ("found why"), and the last
        words ("it isn't on Earth") open a new door. It is also a deliberate misdirection: the viewer now believes the
        answer is out in space, which makes the final twist - "it's coming from you" - land harder.
        (The brief's wording, "...and it's unbelievable", was replaced: "unbelievable" tells the viewer how to feel
        without telling them anything.)                                                              Score 24/25
HOOK 2  (direction 2)  "The scientists found something inside your DNA that shouldn't exist."
        Strong curiosity and personal, but it points the story at biology while this episode is about a signal: the
        payoff would not pay this hook off.                                                         Score 17/25
HOOK 3  (direction 3)  "They thought you were the only survivor. Then a mysterious signal appeared."
        Fits the story, but "They thought..." is a set-up clause: the new information only arrives in the second
        sentence, about 2 s in.                                                                     Score 20/25

RETENTION
- 0.0 s: the monitor with his photo and the pattern, impact, PART 3 / THEY FOUND SOMETHING. 0.12 s: the first words.
- A cut at 1.4 s and at 2.6 s; then a new shot every 1.2-2.6 s for the rest of the film.
- Each scene raises the scale: a monitor, a 40-metre timeline across the lab, Earth from orbit, deep space - then the
  signal reverses and one continuous dive brings the viewer from space, through the atmosphere, over the city and down
  through the facility roof onto him.
- The signal has one recognisable signature everywhere - three short pulses and one long, in the picture and in the
  sound - so the viewer recognises it when it starts coming from him.

ENDING
"Because the signal isn't coming from space anymore... it's coming from you." The camera pushes into his face, a bass
impact lands on "you", the picture cuts to black and the sound to silence. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT? on black. Nothing is
explained: not what the signal is, not what the symbol means, not why it comes from him.
