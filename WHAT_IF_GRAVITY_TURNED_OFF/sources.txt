WHAT IF GRAVITY TURNED OFF FOR 10 SECONDS? - SOURCES, FACT TABLE AND HONESTY NOTES
=====================================================================================

This Short is a HYPOTHETICAL scenario. Gravity cannot be switched off; nothing in it happened. The video says so on
screen ("A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO - SHOWN IN SLOW MOTION", "ILLUSTRATION - NOT TO SCALE"). What it tries to get right is
the physics it uses: inertia (things keep the motion they had), Earth's rotation, lift on an aircraft, friction on
tyres, and falling when gravity returns.

ASSUMPTION OF THE SCENARIO: only Earth's pull on everything at the surface disappears for 10 s and then returns.
Earth itself stays intact and keeps turning. (Removing ALL gravity would also release the pressure inside Earth and the
Sun - a very different, far more destructive scenario that the video does not attempt; see section 5.)

3. THE ONE CALCULATION IN THE VIDEO
-----------------------------------
A point on the equator moves at v = 2 pi R / T = 2 pi x 6,378,137 m / 86,164 s = 465 m/s (1,674 km/h) [1].
If nothing pulls a resting object toward Earth, it keeps that velocity in a straight line (inertia) [4] while the
ground keeps turning. After t seconds its height above the ground is sqrt(R^2 + (v t)^2) - R ~ (v t)^2 / 2R.
For t = 10 s: (4,650 m)^2 / (2 x 6,378,137 m) = 1.69 m. The same geometry gives Hewitt's "5 m drop per 8 km of
tangent" [8]: 8,000^2 / (2 x 6.378e6) = 5.0 m. Equivalently, relative to the ground the object accelerates "upward"
at omega^2 R = 0.0339 m/s^2 (the centrifugal term that normally reduces apparent gravity slightly at the equator).
Away from the equator the effect is smaller (it scales with cos^2 of the latitude for the vertical part), which is why
the narration says "near the equator".
The animation uses exactly this: everything that was resting rises 1.7 m by the end of the ten seconds.


4. CONFIRMED vs ESTIMATED vs SPECULATION
----------------------------------------
CONFIRMED (textbook physics and measured values):
- Earth's equatorial rotation speed (~465 m/s, ~1,670 km/h) [1] [2] [3]; inertia [4] [9]; lift vs weight [5] [6];
  friction proportional to normal force [7]; the 1.7 m drift follows from [1] by simple geometry [8].
ESTIMATED (used only for the look; numbers not stated in the narration):
- How fast people were moving up at the switch (a walking step, a jump, standing still: 0.03-0.26 m/s; a jumping
  basketball player 2 m/s). So people end up 2-4 m up, and the falls are shown as hard but survivable landings.
- Car suspension: a car's springs are squeezed by its weight; with no weight they would push the body up. We used
  0.3-0.45 m/s. Real values depend on the springs and dampers.
- The airliner climbing at about 1 g assumes lift was exactly balancing weight at the switch [5] [6]; real pilots
  would react; the video shows only the first few seconds.
- The fountain: the jets keep rising as straight columns (inertia); real jets would break into drops and spray.
SPECULATION / NOT SHOWN:
- The air. The atmosphere's own pressure is normally balanced by gravity; switch gravity off and that balance is gone,
  so air would start to move too. How strong the effects would be at street level within 10 s depends on assumptions
  we could not verify in a reliable source, so the video makes no claim about it (no wind storm, no "the atmosphere
  flies away"). It does not claim the atmosphere would disappear.
- The oceans: like the fountain's basin water, resting water would drift up with everything else; not discussed.
- Injuries: the video stays non-graphic; nobody is shown hurt. Real falls from 2-6 m can be dangerous.


5. FACTS CHECKED BUT CUT FOR TIME
---------------------------------
- At 45 degrees latitude the surface moves at about 1,180 km/h [2]; the drift there would be smaller and partly sideways.
- Astronauts on the International Space Station float not because there is no gravity there (it is about 90 % of
  surface gravity) but because they are in continuous free fall around Earth - the reason NASA prefers the word
  "microgravity". (NASA's own wording was not retrieved directly; secondary sources agree.)
- Many popular articles about "losing gravity for 5 seconds" claim instant catastrophes (the atmosphere and oceans
  flying off, buildings exploding). Those depend on assuming that ALL gravity, including Earth's self-gravity, vanishes;
  they were not used here.


6. WHAT IS ILLUSTRATIVE
-----------------------
- The whole event, the city (no real place), the shop names (LUNA CAFE, HARBOUR BAKERY, PAGE & SPINE BOOKS, FRESH
  MARKET), the newspaper and every character are invented. People are stylised figures; nobody real is depicted.
- The globe and its "straight line vs. curved ground" diagram: the Earth's turn is sped up enormously so the effect can
  be seen; labelled ILLUSTRATION - NOT TO SCALE.
- Slow motion: scenes 1-3 and 5 are slowed down (labelled); the countdown runs at roughly real time.
