WHAT IF YOU WERE THE ONLY MAN ALIVE?
A 30-second vertical Short, made entirely with code
===================================================

1080 x 1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 30.0 s, H.264 + AAC. A hypothetical, wry story told in the second person ("you"), using
the script and hook from the brief word for word. You wake up and every other man on Earth has disappeared. A
breaking-news alert, the TV, giant screens in a square, a newsroom - and your own face: THE ONLY MAN ALIVE. You splash
your face (not a dream); helicopters circle your tower; scientists study your scans; reporters pack the street. Then the
twist: the world doesn't stop - streets, offices, hospitals, parliament and trains carry on. The payoff: dusk in your
flat, THE LAST MAN ALIVE on the giant screen across the street, and the camera pushes into your eyes. The last frame is
your eyes closing and the first is your eyes opening, so it loops.

Everything you see and hear is generated by code:
- People: a new procedural human builder (build_human in animation.py) made for this film, because the brief asked for
  more realistic characters than the stylised mannequins of the earlier Shorts. Adult proportions; a sculpted head
  (skull, brow ridge, eye sockets, cheekbones, jaw, chin and a nose sculpted into the face); ears; eyeballs with an
  iris, a pupil and a wet coat; upper and lower lids with lashes; eyebrows; lips with smile/open/frown shapes; real
  strand hair grown as Curves (tens of thousands of strands, combed over the skull or falling behind the shoulders);
  subsurface-scattering skin with pores, cheek colour and stubble; lofted bodies under clothes (T-shirts, blazers, lab
  coats, scrubs, skirts, trousers); hands with five fingers. The same builder makes the crowds: people are baked into
  static meshes and instanced by geometry nodes (the square, the reporters, the newsroom, the office, the parliament,
  the train platform, the street).
- Sets, lights and cameras: Blender's Python API (bpy). No stock footage, downloaded models, textures or templates.
- Screens: tools/textures.py (Pillow) draws the phone alert, the invented channel GLOBAL 24's graphics, the square's
  screens, the alert globe, the ID card, the lab's scans, office monitors, signs and THE LAST MAN ALIVE. The ID portrait
  and the news presenter on the screens are rendered by animation.py from its own characters.
- Captions: tools/captions.py (Pillow): word-by-word captions plus four large key captions.
- Music and sound: numpy synthesis in animation.py --stage audio. No samples or recordings.
- Narration: a synthetic voice (Kokoro-82M, voice "bm_george"), made by tools/voiceover.py.


FILES
-----
  script.txt          the narration (the brief's script, 87 words) with measured start/end times
  hooks.txt           the brief's three hooks with scores, the selected hook, retention notes and the ending
  storyboard.txt      scene-by-scene storyboard (generated from scene_timing.json by tools/voiceover.py)
  animation.py        the whole production in one Blender script: people, sets, animation, cameras, lighting, render,
                      audio, edit and encode. Objects are organised in named collections (Flat, City, Chars, Square,
                      Street, News, Lab, Office, Hosp, Parl, Train, Booth, FX, Rig, Lights; PeopleT holds the baked
                      crowd people) and built by reusable functions (build_human, head_mesh, hair_strands, lips_object,
                      hand_bm, bake_human, people, instancer, helicopter, make_car, tower_block, screen_mat,
                      camera_shot, pose, walk_along, reach, look_at, ...)
  subtitles.srt       subtitles timed from the synthesised voice (max 3 words)
  sound_design.txt    the sound idea, every cue with its time, and a music guide for re-scoring
  sources.txt         claim table, what is fiction, what was checked (and the hook-3 figure that was not used)
  README.txt          this file
  final_video.mp4     the finished Short

  scene_timing.json   the single source of truth for timing (scenes, shots, narration, titles, key captions)
  render_all.sh       resumable full render in 200-frame chunks
  tools/              voiceover.py, captions.py, textures.py, contact.py
  audio/              narration.wav, mix_with_voice.wav (final mix), mix_no_voice.wav (music + sound only)
  renders/            preview.mp4 (low-resolution preview), final_video_no_voice.mp4 (no narration or captions)
  assets/fonts/       Anton, Old Standard TT, Covered By Your Grace (SIL OFL)


THE FIVE SCENES (18 shots, every one with camera movement)
-----------------------------------------------------------
  0.0-4.0   YOU WAKE UP            Extreme close-up of his eyes opening; the phone's BREAKING NEWS alert (his point of
                                   view); he sits bolt upright. Key caption: EXCEPT YOU.
  4.0-9.85  THE WORLD FINDS OUT    The TV over his shoulder; a square full of women under giant screens; the GLOBAL 24
                                   newsroom; a whole wall of screens showing his face. Key caption: THE ONLY MAN ALIVE.
  9.85-18.2 THE SEARCH             The bathroom mirror; helicopters circling his tower; scientists at a wall of scans
                                   (Y CHROMOSOME: PRESENT); reporters filling the street, phones raised to his window; a
                                   long-lens shot of him peeking through the curtains.
  18.2-25.3 THE WORLD DOESN'T STOP A busy street with traffic and women walking to work; an office; doctors in a hospital
                                   corridor; a parliament in session; a train pulling in. Key caption: THE WORLD DOESN'T STOP.
  25.3-30.0 THE PAYOFF             Dusk in his flat; THE LAST MAN ALIVE and his face on the giant screen across the
                                   street, through the window behind him; the camera pushes in to his eyes, which close.
                                   Key caption: THE BIGGEST MYSTERY ON IT.


HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3), ffmpeg, Python 3 with Pillow and numpy. The voice step needs Python
with kokoro-onnx and soundfile, plus kokoro-v1.0.onnx and voices-v1.0.bin. In this repository's Claude Code cloud
sessions the SessionStart hook installs everything and exports $BLENDER, $SHORTS_PY, $KOKORO_PY and $KOKORO_DIR.

  cd WHAT_IF_YOU_WERE_THE_ONLY_MAN_ALIVE
  $SHORTS_PY tools/textures.py                      # screen graphics -> build/tex/ (animation.py also runs it if missing)
  $KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav, subtitles.srt, script.txt, storyboard.txt
  $SHORTS_PY tools/captions.py                      # caption images (the edit stage also runs it if missing)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 26,102,182,594,760,890
  ./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
  ./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/ (resumable)
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                  # -> final_video.mp4
  $BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice       # -> renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4

The first render of any kind also renders the ID portrait and the news presenter into build/tex/ (build/tex/feeds_ok
marks them done; delete it to render them again after changing the characters).

To edit in CapCut instead: render the frames, run the audio stage, import renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4 (or the
frames plus audio/mix_no_voice.wav), add audio/narration.wav and use subtitles.srt for the captions.

RENDER TIMES (4 CPU cores, no GPU, Cycles CPU)
----------------------------------------------
  ID portrait + presenter feed (once)          under 1 min (rendered automatically before the first frame)
  Look-dev stills (270x480, 4 samples)         about 3-6 s per frame, plus about 1.5 min of scene building per run
                                               (the people, their strand hair and the baked crowds are built every run)
  Preview, 900 frames (270x480, 4 samples)     26 min (about 1.5 s per frame)
  Final, 900 frames (540x960, 6 samples)       1 h 49 min, about 7 s per frame on average, in a single background run
  Audio stage                                  about 8 s
  Edit stage (final_video.mp4)                 1 min 53 s
  Edit stage --no-voice                        1 min 40 s

  final_video.mp4                   1080x1920, 30 fps, 30.0 s, H.264 + AAC 48 kHz, 29.7 MB, -13.9 LUFS (peak -2.9 dBFS)
  renders/final_video_no_voice.mp4  1080x1920, 30 fps, 30.0 s, H.264 + AAC 48 kHz, 30.2 MB, -13.7 LUFS (peak -2.0 dBFS)


HOW REALISTIC ARE THE PEOPLE? (limitations)
------------------------------------------
- They are much closer to real people than the mannequins in this repository's earlier Shorts - real proportions, small
  eyes with irises, a modelled nose and lips, strand hair, subsurface skin - but they are procedural, built only from
  Python and shapes, without scanned faces, sculpting by hand or a facial rig. Faces are smooth and simplified, clothes
  are shaped shells rather than simulated cloth, and joints are rotated parts rather than a skinned skeleton, so
  elbows, knees and shoulders can look segmented close up. Shots are framed so the faces read best (close-ups with soft
  light and shallow focus) and bodies are mostly seen in motion or at a distance.
- Crowds use the same builder at a lower level of detail (a hair shell instead of strands, no subdivision).


QUALITY CHECK (against the brief)
---------------------------------
- No introduction: frame 1 is his eyes opening; the BREAKING NEWS alert is on screen by 1.5 s; the selected hook
  (Hook 2) is the first line.
- Fast cuts in the first 10 s (8 shots); breaking-news stings, whooshes and glitches on the news cuts.
- Captions large and readable: Anton, white with a black outline, active word amber, max 3 words, about 64% down;
  key captions larger and amber.
- Music intensity rises from 4 s to 18 s as the world finds out, then flips to light and ordinary for the twist.
- Strong visual payoff: THE LAST MAN ALIVE on the giant screen behind him; the final shot pushes into his eyes and the
  first shot is his eyes opening, so the Short loops.
- More realistic characters: see above.


ACCURACY AND HONESTY
--------------------
- Fiction, labelled on screen ("A HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO"); the script is the brief's. The only real-world fact on screen
  ("Y CHROMOSOME: PRESENT" on a lab monitor) is basic biology (sources.txt).
- The channel, places and people are invented; nobody real is depicted.
