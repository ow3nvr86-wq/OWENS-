THE CUBAN MISSILE CRISIS (OCTOBER 1962) - SCRIPT (about 39 s, narrated by George)
------------------------------------------------------------
In October, nineteen sixty-two, an American spy plane found Soviet missiles in Cuba, ninety miles from Florida.

On October twenty-second, President Kennedy went on television, and announced a naval quarantine around Cuba.

Soviet ships were sailing towards the line.

Beneath the waves, a Soviet submarine carried a nuclear torpedo, and American ships were hunting it.

Its officer, Vasili Arkhipov, is credited with refusing to let it fire.

On October twenty-eighth, Khrushchev agreed to remove the missiles, if America promised not to invade Cuba.

In a secret deal, America also agreed to remove its missiles from Turkey.

Many historians call it the closest the world has come to nuclear war.
------------------------------------------------------------
FACT NOTES
- U-2 photos 14/15 Oct (sources differ, so the video says "October 1962"); ExComm met 16 Oct; Kennedy's address and the quarantine 22 Oct;
  Khrushchev announced removal on 28 Oct in exchange for a US pledge not to invade Cuba (History.com, State Dept Office of the Historian).
- Secret deal: the Jupiter missiles in Turkey were removed in April 1963; the arrangement stayed secret for about 25 years (Wilson Center, NPR).
- B-59 / Arkhipov: the sub's crew had lost contact with Moscow; its commander reportedly wanted to fire the nuclear torpedo; Arkhipov's
  refusal is "credited" with averting a launch (account differs between sources, hence the hedged wording).
- "90 miles from Florida" is the commonly cited distance from Key West to Cuba. The quarantine line is drawn as an approximate arc.
- "13 days" is 16-28 October. The range ring to Washington is schematic (medium-range missiles on Cuba could reach it).
