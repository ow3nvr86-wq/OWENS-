# CENTRALIA: THE TOWN THAT BURNED FROM WITHIN

A 60-second, vertical (1080×1920, 30 fps) dark-documentary YouTube Short about Centralia, Pennsylvania, the town
built over an underground coal-mine fire that has burned since 1962. **Every image and every sound is generated by
code.**

- **Visuals:** Blender's Python API (`bpy`) builds the terrain, about 400 period houses, the church, the colliery, cars,
  pedestrians, the burning mine, the cut-away geology, the sinkhole, fire, smoke and embers, and the camera moves.
- **Audio:** numpy synthesises the music and sound effects.
- **Narration:** a synthetic voice (Kokoro-82M, `bm_george`).

There is no stock footage, no downloaded model or animation, and no recorded audio.

## The five scenes

| Time | Scene | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 s | **The hook** | A quiet 1960s street at night. The camera sinks toward the asphalt as it splits open, glowing orange, with smoke and sparks. It plunges into a vast burning coal seam underground. |
| 5-17 s | **The town before** | Golden-hour aerial of Centralia in 1962: frame houses, shops, the onion-domed church on the hill and the colliery breaker. Then street level, with 1950s cars and people walking. Finally the town dump, an old strip-mine pit, where burning trash reaches an opening into a coal seam. |
| 17-31 s | **The fire spreads** | A cut-away of the ground under the town: rock beds, two coal seams with room-and-pillar workings, and the fire front creeping west under the streets. Inside the mine, a flushing borehole pours slurry while the fire glows past it. A doll's-house cut of a home shows hot ground below and gas seeping into the basement. |
| 31-46 s | **The abandonment** | February 1981: a back yard collapses into a smoking hole (no person is shown). Then an aerial time-lapse of the buy-out: houses vanish, vegetation takes over the lots, and the sun sweeps across. Last, the cracked, steaming highway. |
| 46-60 s | **The final reveal** | Above the empty town, the camera descends past the edge of the ground into the cut-away: the fire burns in patches far beyond the town. At night, the camera pulls back over the last lights and smoking vents, fades to black, and shows **CENTRALIA, PENNSYLVANIA / THE TOWN THAT BURNED FROM WITHIN.** |

## How it is built (the interesting parts)

- **Terrain and town.**
  - A numpy Perlin/fBm height field (2 m grid over the town) carries the strip-mine pit, the culm banks and the church hill.
  - Four procedural house types (gable-front frame house, double house, cottage, brick commercial block) are built with bmesh. Geometry Nodes instances them on about 400 lots, with per-instance siding colour and lit windows.
  - The same instancer drives the demolition time-lapse: each house's scale goes to zero at its own time.
- **The fire.** The coal material shows a glowing crack network (Voronoi edges) behind a moving "burn front": the object's `burn` property, read in the shader. The cut-away face is a single shader that reads a per-vertex `depth` attribute. It draws:
  - soil, sandstone and shale beds;
  - two coal seams with mine rooms;
  - the moving fire front;
  - heated, fractured rock above it.
- **Particles.** Smoke, steam, gas, flames and embers are deterministic camera-facing billboards. Each particle's position, size, age and opacity is a closed-form function of time, so any frame renders identically, in any order. The flame and smoke shaders turn the per-particle colour attribute (seed, age, heat, opacity) into turbulent, rising shapes.
- **The crack** in scene 1 is a ribbon mesh along three jagged paths. Its cross-section (lips, steep walls, deep V) widens frame by frame over a flickering under-light. **The sinkhole** is a polar ground patch whose inner ring opens and slumps into a shaft lit from below.
- **Cameras.** Each shot has its own keyframed rig with Track-To and depth of field, bound by timeline markers.
- **Look.**
  - Each shot has a fixed look, applied once before that shot renders: sky, sun or moon, haze, light shafts, exposure, grade, materials and visibility.
  - Fire light comes from flickering point lights. Emissive surfaces are excluded from light sampling, which made render times 2-3× faster.
- **Edit.** Blender's Video Sequencer upscales the frames 2×, adds the vignette, film grain, dips, flashes, titles and word-group captions, and encodes H.264 + AAC.

## Rendering it yourself

Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3) and ffmpeg. The voice step needs Python with `kokoro-onnx` and
`soundfile`, plus the files `kokoro-v1.0.onnx` and `voices-v1.0.bin` from the kokoro-onnx releases. In this repo's
Claude Code cloud sessions, the SessionStart hook installs all of it and exports `$BLENDER`, `$KOKORO_PY` and `$KOKORO_DIR`.

```bash
cd Centralia_Short
$KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav + subtitles.srt + narration.txt + scene_timing.md
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 60,120,300   # look-dev stills
./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/  (resumable)
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                       # renders/centralia_final.mp4
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice            # renders/centralia_no_voice.mp4
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --out renders/preview_frames --output preview.mp4
```

**Render budget (4 CPU cores, no GPU).**

| Render | Frames | Time per frame | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final, 540×960 at 8 samples (OIDN denoised, upscaled in the edit) | 1,728 (3D) | about 12-17 s; mine and fire shots are the slowest | about 7.5 hours (measured) |
| Preview, 270×480 at 4 samples | 1,728 (3D) | about 3 s | about 1.5 hours |

The last 2.4 s (the title) is drawn by the edit over black. On a desktop GPU, Cycles renders this many times faster.

**To change the narration timing**, edit the times in `scene_timing.json`, re-run `tools/voiceover.py`, and then
re-run the audio and edit stages. Re-render the 3D frames only if you change the shot boundaries.

**To record your own voice-over** in CapCut, import `renders/centralia_no_voice.mp4`. Read `narration.txt`, using the
start times in `scene_timing.md` or `subtitles.srt`.

## Accuracy and honesty notes

- Every fact on screen and in the narration is checked against the Pennsylvania DEP's Centralia chronology and FAQ,
  plus cross-checks. See `research_sources.md` for the claim-by-claim table.
- Where sources disagree, the wording is hedged: "most likely" for the cause, "over a thousand people" for the
  population, and no year on the $42 M relocation money.
- The brief's ending line was adapted to **"A whole town almost disappeared..."**, because five residents (2020) and a
  few buildings remain. "The fire kept burning" is supported: the state still monitors an active fire.
- These are illustrations, not documentary footage or literal records:
  - the town layout;
  - the colliery;
  - the mine geometry;
  - the cut-away (labelled **"ILLUSTRATION, NOT TO SCALE"**);
  - the sinkhole;
  - the demolition time-lapse (in reality it took years).
- People are stylised mannequins. The 1981 sinkhole is shown without any person, and no injury or real individual is
  depicted.
