# Research and sources: Centralia, Pennsylvania

Researched October 2026. The primary source is the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection (DEP), the
state agency that monitors the fire. Its Centralia pages include a chronology and a 14-page FAQ PDF from the Bureau of
Abandoned Mine Reclamation. Other sources were used for cross-checks and for figures the DEP does not give.

## Changed from the brief for accuracy

- The brief's closing line, "An entire town disappeared... and beneath it, the fire kept burning", was changed to **"A whole town almost disappeared..."**. A handful of residents remain (5 in 2020), and a few buildings still stand, including the Ukrainian church on the hill and the municipal building. The second half of the line was kept: the research supports it.
- "Nothing put it out" is used rather than "it can't be put out". DEP says that with "a very large and very expensive effort" the fire *could* be excavated, but the cost is beyond its program's capacity.

## Facts researched but not used (to keep the Short at 60 s)

- The 1979 gasoline-tank reading of 172 °F at the mayor's filling station (4, 9).
- 1983: an Office of Surface Mining study put the cost of extinguishing the fire at $663 million (2).
- 1992: the state invoked eminent domain on all properties; the ZIP code 17927 was discontinued (2002 per USPS / Wikipedia, 2003 per DEP).
- 2013: the last residents settled with the state and may stay for life (4, 5).
- Route 61 was closed in 1992-93; the abandoned stretch ("Graffiti Highway") was covered with earth in April 2020 (2, 5).

## Illustrative, not literal

- **Town layout.** A simplified grid of procedural houses in period styles (frame houses, double houses, brick commercial blocks). It is not a survey of the real borough.
- **The colliery breaker.** It represents the town's coal-mining identity. Most large-scale mining near Centralia had ended before 1962 (3).
- **The mine and cross-section.** Seam depths, the room-and-pillar layout, the fire-front speed and the glow are diagrammatic. They are labelled "ILLUSTRATION, NOT TO SCALE". The real fire burns in the Buck Mountain bed and possibly overlying beds, at depths of up to about 90 m (300 ft).
- **The sinkhole.** The 1981 yard collapse is shown without any person. Its size is approximate.
- **The demolition.** It is shown as a time-lapse. In reality it took place over years (mostly 1985-1991).
