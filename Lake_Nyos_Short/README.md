# LAKE NYOS: THE INVISIBLE KILLER

A 60-second, vertical (1080×1920, 30 fps) dark-documentary YouTube Short about the night of 21 August 1986. That
night a crater lake in Cameroon released a cloud of carbon dioxide that killed about 1,746 people. **Every image and
every sound is generated by code.**

- **Visuals:** Blender's Python API (`bpy`) builds:
  - the volcanic highlands, the crater and its 208 m deep lake;
  - the underwater world;
  - the villages in the valley below;
  - the eruption;
  - the CO2 cloud, driven by a gas-flow simulation;
  - the degassing raft;
  - all particles, lighting and camera moves.
- **Audio:** numpy synthesises the music and sound effects.
- **Narration:** a synthetic voice (Kokoro-82M, `bm_george`).

There is no stock footage, no downloaded model or animation, and no recorded audio.

## The six scenes

| Time | Scene | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| 0-6 s | **The hook** | Dusk aerial over a village in a mountain valley, with oil lamps lit in the houses. The camera flies up the valley toward the crater, and a blue lake appears beyond the rim. Night falls and mist gathers over the water. |
| 6-15 s | **The lake** | Skimming over the calm crater lake at blue hour, then plunging under the surface. As the camera sinks, a scientific visualisation of dissolved CO2 (tiny glowing specks) thickens toward the dark bottom. |
| 15-30 s | **The eruption** | The 208 m deep floor, where springs feed CO2-charged water into the bottom layer. The camera rises through the layered water to the surface. Then night on the lake: the surface boils, a column of water, foam and mist bursts upward, and surges race outward. |
| 30-43 s | **The invisible threat** | From above, a translucent visualisation of the cloud pours over the low northern rim and races down the valley like a river. Down in the village, the cloud flows between the houses and the oil lamps gutter and go out one by one, while the houses on the ridge stay lit. |
| 43-53 s | **The aftermath** | The empty village at dawn, with mist and silence; the camera rises above the valley. Then today: the degassing raft on the lake, its fountain venting the gas. |
| 53-60 s | **The final reveal** | Under the raft, the camera follows the pipe down into the dark. Gas still collects at the bottom and is drawn up the pipe. Fade to black: **LAKE NYOS / THE INVISIBLE DISASTER**. |

## How it is built (the interesting parts)

- **Terrain.**
  - Elliptical crater, bathymetry with a flat 208 m deep plain, and crater walls whose lowest point is the northern spillway.
  - A valley carved along a polyline, descending north and branching.
  - Rolling grassland built from numpy Perlin fBm noise.
  - One 467,000-vertex grid, 5 m spacing over the crater and the valley.
- **Lake surface.** A grid inside the shoreline, re-displaced every frame:
  - calm ripples;
  - the boiling bulge and travelling surge rings of the overturn;
  - foam as a mesh attribute;
  - today's fountain disturbance.

  One shader shows two faces: dark reflective water from above, and from below a bright, rippling "Snell's window".
- **The gas cloud (a real simulation).** `run_gas_sim()` solves the 2-D shallow-water equations for a dense gas on the scene's own terrain, on a 20 m grid. It uses:
  - reduced gravity g' = g·(ρCO2 − ρair)/ρCO2;
  - a well-balanced finite-volume scheme (hydrostatic reconstruction with Rusanov fluxes);
  - Manning friction;
  - slow dilution.

  The cloud starts over the lake and is fed for a few minutes. It fills the crater, spills over the lowest part of the rim and runs down the valley. It reaches the village floor about a minute after release at ~35 m deep, and stays below the ridge where Upper Nyos stands. Each frame turns the result into a translucent sheet plus drifting wisps. Each house's oil lamp reads the simulated gas thickness at its position: the lamp gutters and goes out as the cloud arrives.
- **CO2 "dots".** A deterministic field of tiny emissive billboards whose density follows a depth profile (sparse near the surface, dense toward the bottom). Streams rise from vents on the lake floor. Today's version is sparse and is drawn up the degassing pipe.
- **Particles.** Mist, spray and gas wisps are camera-facing billboards. Every particle's state is a closed-form function of time, so any frame renders identically, in any order.
- **Look.**
  - Each shot has a fixed look (sky, sun or moon, haze, light shafts, exposure, grade, visibility, material values), applied once before that shot renders.
  - The hook animates the light draining away.
  - Emissive surfaces are excluded from light sampling, which is much faster.
- **Edit.** Blender's Video Sequencer upscales the frames 2×. It adds:
  - the vignette, film grain and dips;
  - a cold flash on the plunge and a white flash on the burst;
  - titles and word-group captions.

  It then encodes H.264 + AAC.

## Rendering it yourself

Requirements: Blender 4.2 LTS (tested with 4.2.3) and ffmpeg. The voice step needs Python with `kokoro-onnx` and
`soundfile`, plus the files `kokoro-v1.0.onnx` and `voices-v1.0.bin` from the kokoro-onnx releases. In this repo's
Claude Code cloud sessions, the SessionStart hook installs all of it and exports `$BLENDER`, `$KOKORO_PY` and `$KOKORO_DIR`.

```bash
cd Lake_Nyos_Short
$KOKORO_PY tools/voiceover.py                     # narration.wav + subtitles.srt + narration.txt + scene_timing.md
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --quality preview --stills 60,300,500,850,1060,1100   # look-dev stills
./render_all.sh preview                           # full 270x480 preview -> renders/preview_frames/
./render_all.sh final                             # full 540x960 render  -> renders/frames/  (resumable)
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage audio
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit                       # renders/lake_nyos_final.mp4
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --no-voice            # renders/lake_nyos_no_voice.mp4
$BLENDER -b --factory-startup -P animation.py -- --stage edit --out renders/preview_frames --output preview.mp4
```

The first render builds the gas simulation, which takes about 1 minute, and caches it in `build/gas_sim.npz`. Delete that file to re-run it after changing the terrain or the gas parameters.

**Render budget (4 CPU cores, no GPU).**

| Render | Frames | Time per frame | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final, 540×960 at 8 samples (OIDN denoised, upscaled in the edit) | 1,722 (3D) | about 13-15 s | about 7 hours |
| Preview, 270×480 at 4 samples | 1,722 (3D) | about 3 s | about 1.5-2 hours |

The last 2.6 s (the title) is drawn by the edit over black. On a desktop GPU, Cycles renders this many times faster.

**To change the narration timing**, edit the times in `scene_timing.json`, re-run `tools/voiceover.py`, and then
re-run the audio and edit stages. Re-render the 3D frames only if you change the shot boundaries.

**To record your own voice-over** in CapCut, import `renders/lake_nyos_no_voice.mp4`. Read `narration.txt`, using the
start times in `scene_timing.md` or `subtitles.srt`.

## Accuracy and honesty notes

- Every fact on screen and in the narration is checked against the 1987 field study in *Science* (Kling et al.) and
  later scientific summaries. See `research_sources.md` for the claim-by-claim table.
- No trigger for the overturn is named, because it is still unknown. The death toll is "about 1,746". No distance is
  given for the gas's reach, because sources disagree.
- **The gas could not be seen.** Every on-screen gas effect is labelled **"VISUALISATION: THE REAL GAS WAS INVISIBLE"**.
- The underwater CO2 specks are labelled **"SCIENTIFIC VISUALISATION"**. Dissolved CO2 does not glow.
- These are illustrations, not documentary footage or literal records:
  - the terrain, crater and village layout;
  - the eruption's shape and location;
  - the moonlit look of the night;
  - the gas flow, which is simulated but compressed in time, with simplified volumes.
- **No people and no animals are shown.** The loss of life is conveyed through empty houses, lamps going out and
  silence.
