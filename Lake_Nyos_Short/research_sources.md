# Research and sources: Lake Nyos, Cameroon (21 August 1986)

Researched October 2026. The main source is the original field study published six months after the disaster
(Kling et al., *Science*, 1987). Its authors were on site from 27 August 1986. Later scientific summaries and
reputable journalism were used for cross-checks and for figures settled later, such as the official death toll and
the degassing pipes.

## Changed or hedged for accuracy

- **The brief's hook is kept word for word.** The brief's ending line "The most terrifying part? The danger was completely invisible." is also kept, with the caveat above.
- **"Cold bottom water" was removed from the script.** Sources disagree on whether Nyos's deep water is colder or slightly warmer than the water above it, so the line now says "the lake's bottom water".
- **No trigger is named.** The cause of the overturn is still debated.
- **The death toll is "About 1,746".** The commonly cited official figure is used, with "about" because the 1987 field study said "at least 1700".
- **No distance is given for how far the gas killed.** Sources range from 10 km (Kling 1987) to ~25 km (later accounts).

## What is illustrative (not literal)

- **The landscape.** The crater, the valley north of the lake and the village layout are procedural and simplified. They are not a survey of the real terrain. The houses (mud-brick walls with metal or thatch roofs, compounds, banana plants) represent the Cameroon grassfields in general. People and animals are not shown.
- **The underwater "dots".** They are a scientific visualisation of dissolved CO2: more toward the bottom, few near the surface. They are labelled on screen. Dissolved CO2 does not glow and cannot be seen.
- **The gas cloud.** It is a visualisation, labelled "VISUALISATION: THE REAL GAS WAS INVISIBLE". Its motion comes from a 2-D shallow-water simulation of a dense gas on the scene's own terrain:
  - reduced gravity for CO2 in air;
  - friction from the ground;
  - slow dilution.

  The simulated front moves at roughly 60-80 km/h, compared with reported speeds of 20-50 km/h (Wikipedia) and ~45 mph (Smithsonian). The simulated volume (~0.3 km3 of dense core) is smaller than Kling's 1.2 km3 estimate of all the gas released. The shot is **compressed in time**: about 4 minutes of flow is shown in 13 seconds.
- **The eruption.** The water column and spreading spray are based on the reports: a fountain, surges of ~25 m and the lake level dropping ~1 m. The exact location and shape are illustrative.
- **The overturn shot is lit by moonlight** so it can be seen. The real event happened in the dark, at about 9:30 pm.

## Facts researched but not used (to keep the Short at 60 s)

- 15 August 1984: a smaller gas burst at Lake Monoun, also in Cameroon, killed 37 people (1).
- Survivors woke after 6 to 36 hours. Many had pressure sores, and some had respiratory problems or paralysis (1, 4).
- Upper Nyos, on high ground, was largely spared (4, 6). The video shows its lamps staying lit.
- The lake's natural rock dam is weak. A 2005 UN assessment warned it could fail within 10-20 years (5).
- About 4,000 people fled the area. The worst-hit villages were Nyos, Kam, Cha and Subum (4).
