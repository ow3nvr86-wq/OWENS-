2. VERIFIED TIMELINE
--------------------
1585-86    An earlier, all-male English expedition (Ralph Lane) builds a fort on Roanoke Island, then leaves. [NPS87]
1587 May   About 115-118 colonists sail under John White, governor, intending to settle on Chesapeake Bay. [NPS87, WIKI]
1587 July  The fleet reaches Roanoke Island; the pilot, Simon Fernandes, refuses to carry them further. The colonists
           repair the 1585 fort's houses and build new ones. [NPS87, NCP]
1587       Colonist George Howe is killed in an ambush; the English retaliate and, by mistake, attack friendly
           Croatoans. Food is short and winter is coming. [NPS87]
1587 13 Aug  Manteo, a Croatoan who had worked with the English, is baptised. [NCP]
1587 18 Aug  Virginia Dare, White's granddaughter, is born on Roanoke Island. [NPS87, NCP]
1587 late Aug  At the colonists' request White sails to England for supplies and more settlers (27 Aug per WIKI;
           29 Aug per NPS87; the script says only "sailed to England"). He is the last known English contact. [NCP]
1588       England, threatened by the Spanish Armada, holds its ships at home; a small relief voyage in 1588 is turned back
           by attackers at sea. [NPS90, NCP, WIKI]
1590 Aug   White returns with privateers. At night on 17 August his men see a fire through the woods, "sounded with a
           trumpet a Call, & afterwardes many familiar English tunes of Songs ... but we had no answere." [WHITE90]
1590 18 Aug  They land at daybreak (NCpedia notes it was Virginia Dare's third birthday). They find "CRO" carved on a
           tree, the houses "taken downe", the place "strongly enclosed with a high palisado of great trees", and on
           one of the posts, "the barke taken off, and 5. foote from the ground in fayre Capitall letters was grauen
           CROATOAN without any crosse or signe of distresse." White's own chests have been dug up: books torn, maps
           and pictures rotten with rain, his armour "almost eaten through with rust". [WHITE90, NCP, NPS90]
           The agreed sign: before he left, the colonists were to carve the name of the place they had gone to, and
           a cross over it if they left in distress. [WHITE90]
1590       A storm, a broken cable and lost anchors stop the ships from going on to Croatoan; White never returns. [WHITE90, NPS90]


3. THEORIES (presented in the video as theories, never as conclusions)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- They moved to Croatoan (today's Hatteras Island area), home of Manteo's people, and lived among them. The carved
  word is the main clue; later reports of English-descended Hatteras people (John Lawson, early 1700s) are cited
  for it. [NCPC, NCP, WIKI]
- They moved inland and joined or lived beside other Native communities of the mainland (the colonists had spoken of
  moving "into the main"). Archaeology at "Site X" in Bertie County, chosen after a patch on White's own map was found
  to hide a fort symbol, has turned up English artefacts; its excavators say this does not prove the colonists lived
  there, and other archaeologists dispute it. [WUNC, PBSNC, WIKI]
- Other possibilities discussed by historians: they were killed (a 1609 report and later claims, sources unclear),
  died of hunger or disease (a tree-ring study shows a severe drought), or were lost at sea. [NCP, WIKI]
- Archaeologists are still digging on Hatteras (Cape Creek) and in Bertie County; no find has settled the question. [WUNC, PBSNC]


4. CLAIM-BY-CLAIM CHECK OF THE NARRATION AND TEXT
------------------------------------------------
"An entire colony vanished... and left behind one mysterious word."
    The colony's fate is unknown [all]. The brief's wording "all they left behind was one word" was softened, because
    White also found chests, the palisade and "CRO" on a tree [WHITE90]; the word is the key clue.
"July, 1587." / "More than a hundred English men, women and children landed on Roanoke Island."
    July 1587 [NPS87]. Numbers vary: ~115 [WIKI], 117-118 [NPS87], so "more than a hundred". Women and children [NPS87].
    On screen: "Off the coast of present-day North Carolina".
"They rebuilt an abandoned fort's houses."
    They repaired the 1585 fort and buildings [NPS87].
"But food was short, and clashes with local peoples turned deadly."
    Food shortages and Algonquian attacks strained the colony [NPS87]; George Howe was killed and the English
    retaliation killed Croatoans by mistake [NPS87]. The arrow in the post stands for Howe's death; no attacker is shown.
"Governor John White sailed to England for supplies... leaving his newborn granddaughter, Virginia Dare."
    [NPS87, NCP]. On screen: "Born 18 August 1587".
"But England was at war with Spain. Ships were held back to fight the Armada. One year became three."
    [NPS90, NCP]; 1587 -> 1590.
"August, 1590. White finally returned. At night, his men sounded a trumpet and sang English songs. No one answered."
    [WHITE90] (quoted above).
"The houses were gone. Carved into a post, one word: CROATOAN. And no cross, their agreed sign of distress."
    [WHITE90] (quoted above).
"Croatoan was a nearby island, home to allies. Did they go there? Join Native communities? No one has proven it."
    Croatoan, about 50 miles south, home of Manteo's people, friendly to the English [NCPC, NPS87]. Theories as section 3.
    On screen: "Croatoan: home of Manteo's people", "Theory: they moved to Croatoan", "Theory: they joined Native
    communities", "Excavations: Hatteras Island, Bertie County", "Not proven".
"Archaeologists are still searching... and the forest keeps its secret."
    Excavations continue [WUNC, PBSNC]. The last clause is atmosphere, not a claim.


5. CONFIRMED vs UNCERTAIN
-------------------------
Confirmed: July 1587 arrival; White's departure in 1587; Virginia Dare's birth on 18 August 1587; the Armada delay;
the 1590 return; the trumpet and songs at night; the dismantled houses and palisade; "CROATOAN" on a post and "CRO"
on a tree; no cross.
Uncertain: the exact number of colonists (112-121); White's exact departure day (27 or 29 August); what happened to
the colonists. The video does not say whether they survived, assimilated, moved or died.


6. WHAT IS ARTISTIC RECONSTRUCTION
----------------------------------
- Every face and figure is invented: no portraits of the colonists survive (John White's watercolours show the
  Algonquian people and places, not the 1587 colonists). Clothing follows English dress of the 1580s in general.
- The look of the houses (timber frames, wattle and daub, thatch) is a plausible reconstruction; their real form
  is not known. The palisade, gate posts and the post's size are drawn from White's description, not from evidence.
- The carving's letter forms are drawn by hand to follow "fair capital letters"; the real letters are not recorded.
  The dashed burgundy cross over the word is a graphic showing the sign that was NOT there.
- The map is an illustration in the manner of a 1580s chart, not a survey; the Native towns on it are symbols,
  not located villages.
- The Armada plate is a general image of the 1588 campaign (fire ships, a crescent of Spanish ships).
- The time-lapse of seasons is symbolic: nobody knows when the colonists left the island.
- Native people are shown only as towns on the map: the video does not depict the Roanoke, Croatoan or other
  Algonquian peoples as characters, so as not to invent their likenesses or their part in events.


7. FACTS CUT FOR TIME
---------------------
Manteo's baptism; the mistaken attack on the Croatoans; the second child born (to the Harvie family); the Spanish
searches of 1588; White's 1588 relief attempt; Fernandes' refusal to sail on to the Chesapeake; the "CRO" on the
tree; the Lost Colony's shoreline possibly eroded into the sound; the Dare Stones (dismissed as a hoax).
