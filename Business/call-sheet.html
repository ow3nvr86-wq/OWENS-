Drive Time Call Sheet
6:45 – 7:45am
4:30 – 6:00pm

First eight seconds

Say this, word for word

Never open with the word AI. Open with the symptom. Talk faster than feels natural for the first line, then slow down.

"Hey, is this [his name]?" Wait for yes. Use his name once, never again.

"Good, I'll be quick, I know you're probably driving. I'm [your name], I'm local, out of [city]. I set contractors up so the calls you miss while you're on a job get answered automatically."

"Quick question and then I'll let you go. How many calls do you figure you miss in a normal week?"

Now stop talking. Count to ten if you have to
Whatever number he says is the sale. Everyone says a bigger number than they meant to, and hearing themselves say it out loud does more work than anything you could pitch. Do not fill the silence.

Next two minutes

Five questions, in order

You are not pitching. You are taking notes. Write down his exact words, you will use them later.

  1. "When you miss one, who calls them back, and when?" Usually him, at 9pm, or nobody.
  2. "How many bids do you have sitting out right now with no answer?" This one stings. Let it.
  3. "What's an average job worth to you?" Multiply it by his missed calls later. Don't do it out loud yet.
  4. "What are you using for scheduling and invoicing?" Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or a paper calendar. Just note it.
  5. "What's the part of the week you hate most?" This is the one that sells. Whatever he answers is what you build first. Repeat it back to him before you move on.

If he's engaged, run the demo

"Do me a favor. Call [your demo number] right now and hang up when it rings." Then say nothing. His phone buzzes in four seconds.

This closes deals that no explanation will. It only works if you built it on your own number first, so build that before you dial anybody.

Tap the one he says

What to say back

Never argue. Agree with him first, then ask one more question. The question is what keeps you on the phone.

"Not interested."

"Totally fair. One thing before I go, and then I'm gone. When you miss a call, who calls them back?"

He expected you to push. You didn't, so he answers. Most conversations restart right here.

"I don't have time for this."

"That's exactly why I called. Thirty seconds. How many calls do you miss in a week?"

His objection is your pitch. Say it like you're agreeing with him, because you are.

"How much is it?"

"$750 to set up, $300 a month to run it. But I don't know yet whether you even need it. How many calls are you missing?"

Answer the price instantly and without flinching. Dodging it kills trust. Then get straight back to his problem.

"I already have an answering service."

"Good, that's more than most guys have. Do they text the customer back in twenty seconds, or do they take a message?"

Answering services take messages. Speed is the whole difference and he already knows it.

"My wife answers the phone."

"Then she's the one I should be talking to. Is she still catching them at seven at night?"

Don't fight it. She is a real person doing a real job badly served by a phone. Get her on the line.

"Is this an AI robot thing?"

"It's a text message. That's it. You tell me the wording, I set it up, it sends when you miss a call. Nothing talks to your customer that you didn't write."

He's picturing a robot voice on the phone with his customer. Kill that picture immediately.

"How old are you?"

"Young enough that this is all I do. Call [your demo number] right now and hang up, and I'll show you instead of telling you."

It will come up. Don't apologize, don't oversell, don't lie. Pivot straight to the demo, because working software settles it and nothing else does.

"Just send me some information."

"I'll text you the link right now while we're talking. Is this the best number? ... Sent. I'll call you Thursday same time to see what you thought."

Send it while he's on the phone, and book the callback before you hang up. "Send me info" with no date is a polite no.

Landing it

Ask for one small thing

Don't sell him the whole catalog. One system, one price, one date.

"Here's what I'd do. Pick the thing that's costing you most, and from what you said that's [his answer to question 5]."

"I build that one system, it's live in seven days, $750 to set up and $300 a month after that. If it isn't working in seven days, you don't pay me the setup fee."

"Can I call you Thursday, same time, and we'll start?" Then stop talking again. Same as the opener.

Before you hang up, every time

  1. Book an actual day and time "I'll call you Thursday" is nothing. "Thursday at 6:50" is something.
  2. Text him the site link while he's still on the line Confirm out loud that it arrived.
  3. Write his answer to question 5 in your sheet Word for word. You will open Thursday's call by repeating it back.
  4. Actually call on Thursday Most of your money is in the second and third call. Almost nobody makes them, which is exactly why they work.
Expect this to be slow. Out of 120 dials, roughly 30 to 40 answer, a dozen or so really talk, six or eight agree to a follow-up, and two or three pay. That is a normal week, not a failure. Track it below so you can see it working before the money shows up.
