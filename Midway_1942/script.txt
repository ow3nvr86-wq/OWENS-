THE BATTLE OF MIDWAY (4-7 JUNE 1942) - SCRIPT
Target 34-40 s, about 100 spoken words (numbers written as spoken). One continuous map shot over the Pacific.

HOOK OPTIONS
  A. "Six months after Pearl Harbor, Japan's navy looked unstoppable. Then, in a few minutes, it lost three aircraft carriers."   <- CHOSEN
  B. "In 1942, American codebreakers learned where Japan would strike next. They were told it was a trap for them."
  C. "On June fourth, 1942, the Pacific war turned on one question: where are the Japanese carriers?"

SCRIPT (copy-paste block for the narrator)
------------------------------------------------------------
Six months after Pearl Harbor, Japan's navy looked unstoppable.

Then, in a few minutes, it lost three aircraft carriers.

Japan planned a trap at Midway, a tiny island in the Pacific. But American codebreakers had read its secret messages.

On June fourth, nineteen forty-two, the two fleets met.

American torpedo planes attacked first, and were nearly wiped out.

But they pulled Japan's fighters down to the waves. Then dive bombers came out of the clouds.

Three Japanese carriers were hit, and burning, within minutes.

A fourth fought on, but it was sunk too.

Japan never recovered its edge. The tide of the Pacific war had turned.
------------------------------------------------------------
SAY IT AS: "nineteen forty-two", "June fourth".

FACT NOTES (what is solid, and what was softened)
- Japan's carriers Akagi, Kaga and Soryu were fatally hit by US dive bombers on 4 June; Hiryu was found and sunk later
  (NHHC, navy.mil). Four Japanese fleet carriers were lost; the US lost the carrier Yorktown.
- US torpedo squadrons suffered terrible losses (Torpedo 8: one aircraft survived of 15) and their attacks drew
  Japanese fighters low, leaving the dive bombers unopposed (Defense Media Network; Warfare History Network).
- Station HYPO (Joseph Rochefort) read Japanese naval messages and identified "AF" as Midway. The "water shortage"
  message test is a famous story; historian Craig Symonds says it was meant to convince Washington, not how Rochefort
  learned the target (History.com). The script therefore says only that codebreakers read Japan's messages.
- Exact minute-by-minute times (the "five minutes" of the dive-bomb strike) were not confirmed in the sources checked,
  so the script says "within minutes".
- Death tolls were NOT confirmed in the sources checked (the commonly quoted 3,057 Japanese and 307 American dead need a
  source), so no numbers of dead are narrated.
- "Japan never recovered its edge": a widely shared historians' view that Midway was the turning point; hedged as such.

ANIMATION PLAN (one continuous shot over a real Pacific map, same engine as WW2)
- Hook: Japan's 1942 empire in red across the Pacific; Pearl Harbor flash; a counter "6 MONTHS".
- Midway: the camera pulls to a tiny dot in the middle of the ocean; Japanese fleet arrows from the west.
- Codebreakers: a decoded-message overlay over Hawaii; a blue arrow converging on Midway.
- June 4: two fleets meet; torpedo planes sweep in and fall; the dive bombers drop from above.
- Three red carrier icons burn; the fourth flares and sinks; the red front pulls back.
- End: the map shifts from red to blue westward; title card.
