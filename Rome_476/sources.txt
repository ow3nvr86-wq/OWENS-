2. CLAIM-BY-CLAIM CHECK
"You were taught Rome fell in 476."  476 is the traditional date for the end of the Western Empire: Odoacer deposed
    Romulus Augustulus. [BRIT1]
"That's wrong."  The hook's framing: the Eastern Roman Empire (called 'Byzantine' only by later historians; its
    people called themselves Romans) continued until 1453, so 476 ended only the western half. [BRIT1, BRIT3]
"At its peak, Rome ruled from Britain to Egypt: three continents"  Europe, Africa and Asia. [BRIT1, AWMC]
"and maybe one in four people on Earth."  Estimates of the empire's share of the world's population at its height run
    from about 20% to 30% [MIL, SCH]; hence "maybe" in the narration and "~1 IN 4" on screen.
"In 117 AD, under Emperor Trajan, it hit its biggest size ever."  Greatest extent at Trajan's death in 117, after the
    conquest of Dacia and the advance into Armenia and Mesopotamia. [BRIT1, BRIT2]; the map is the AWMC 117 extent.
"Then it cracked. Civil wars. Plague. Invaders on every border."  The Antonine Plague (from 165) and the Plague of
    Cyprian (from 249); the Crisis of the Third Century (235-284) with its usurpers and civil wars, and pressure on the
    Rhine, Danube and eastern frontiers. [BRIT1, BRIT2]
"So Rome split in two."  The division into East and West became permanent at the death of Theodosius I in 395
    (on screen: 395 AD / THE SPLIT). [BRIT1, BRIT2]
"In 476, the West collapsed. But the East kept going."  [BRIT1]
"For almost a thousand more years, until 1453."  476 to 1453 is 977 years. [BRIT3]
"So when did Rome really fall?"  A question, not a claim.

3. NUANCE (not narrated)
- Historians also date the West's end to 480 (death of Julius Nepos), and Roman successor states lasted past 1453
  (Trebizond to 1461). The film uses the two standard dates.
- Earlier divisions of rule (Diocletian's tetrarchy, 293) preceded the permanent split of 395.

4. MAPS AND RECONSTRUCTION
- Coastlines are real (Natural Earth). The empire of AD 117 and the roads are AWMC data.
- The empire of AD 395 is the 117 extent minus the lands given up by then: Dacia (c. 271-275), the Agri Decumates
  (c. 260) and the lands beyond the Euphrates (363, 387). The East-West line follows the boundary between the
  prefectures of Italy and Illyricum (the Drina valley; the Altars of the Philaeni in Libya); it is approximate.
- In the scale scene the AD 117 extent stands for "at its peak".
- Arrows, the plague shadow and the burning of the West are symbols, not maps of real campaigns or epidemics.
- The legionary wears an Imperial Gallic helmet (1st-2nd century AD) and stands for Rome throughout. Trajan's bust is a
  stylised drawing after his portrait type (the straight fringe of comma-shaped locks). Constantinople is an
  illustration (walls, towers, domes, a column), not a plan.
